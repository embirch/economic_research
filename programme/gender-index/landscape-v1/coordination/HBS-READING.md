## Findings that change our approach

There are 58 plotted entries and 18 narrative entries. Entries 8/56 reuse one survey for different outcomes; 63/75 share the Nature survey lineage. The weighted synthesis uses total sample sizes for 54 entries, rather than world-population weights. Figure 2 compares heterogeneous studies over time. SimilarWeb measures estimated website traffic; Figure 3 averages domains without traffic weighting. Figure 6 weights ages within each gender and shades age-bucket dispersion. Appendix B already analyses tool categories. Table 2 organises mechanisms around familiarity, usefulness, institutional support, social legitimacy and trust/risk.

These distinctions matter more than a headline count. Our interpretation and required work are below; they are programme decisions, not additional results from the paper.

## How this foundation governs the landscape

**Make coverage auditable.** Treat all 76 numbered entries as the baseline reconciliation checklist, including the narrative evidence. An entry must remain traceable even if it maps to a previously catalogued publication or sample. Give publications, datasets, waves and outcome estimates separate identifiers. Then add distinct outside sources. Do not substitute a larger card count for this accounting, or discard a useful study because its microdata are unavailable. The [coverage correction](COVERAGE-RECONCILIATION.md) specifies the dispositions to record.

**Keep different questions separate.** For each proposed indicator, write the denominator and observation unit before comparing values: population adoption, use among people aware of a product, use among workers, audience composition, repeated visits and time spent are different quantities. Tag general AI and generative AI separately. Attach the original question wording, recall window, fieldwork dates, population exclusions and source gender coding. Missing information stays missing. This permits a broader global evidence library without manufacturing a harmonised global rate.

**Do not turn a synthesis into population inference.** Before reusing a pooled result, reproduce its inclusion rules, source weights and arithmetic, then investigate repeated samples and sensitivity to large studies. Keep source-weighted synthesis distinct from a population estimate. For temporal claims, prefer repeated comparable measurements; assess how sample and question changes could produce the apparent movement. Do not infer an individual's trajectory from a sequence of unrelated surveys.

**Use the mechanisms to organise questions, not assign causes.** Build a claims map that distinguishes proposed explanations, descriptive associations and experimental evidence. Look for contrary and null results as well as supportive ones. Within-occupation comparisons can help investigate composition, but cannot by themselves eliminate selection or prove a mechanism. Preserve the distinction between institutional constraints and individual preferences. Do not infer that a gender difference is a skills deficit, or that reducing it necessarily improves welfare.

**Preserve meaningful disagreement.** When platform and survey results appear inconsistent, first compare their populations, access channels, account restrictions, time windows and gender classification. Record whether a proposed explanation was tested or is only plausible. Avoid choosing whichever estimate best fits a preferred narrative. Report unavailable or unclassified demographic coverage where documented rather than allocating it to binary categories.

## What would make our original research additive?

For Emily's SimilarWeb candidate, a fresh chart of gender by tool or category is insufficient evidence of novelty. Scope a specific unanswered question against the main analysis and Appendix B. A possible decomposition would distinguish changes within a fixed set of tools from changes in their traffic weights, with entry/exit and domain redirects handled explicitly. Assess this only after checking the available export, overlap between audiences, demographic estimation, device coverage and reuse terms. It remains a candidate, not an authorised study or a confirmed contribution.

For the parked news/scandal/advertising/offers idea, define events independently of observed spikes and check whether temporal resolution supports the proposed response window. Compare gender-specific levels as well as shares: changes in one group's activity alter the other's share mechanically. Investigate substitution between tools, seasonality, launches and coincident campaigns. A descriptive alignment is useful if labelled honestly; a causal interpretation needs a defensible comparison and separate identification work. Trust or legitimacy evidence may help frame hypotheses, but traffic cannot reveal a person's motivation.

For the index more broadly, keep participation, intensity, opportunity, exposure and realised outcomes distinct. Our contribution should be traceable original calculations and explanations of heterogeneity, with a clear statement of what each analysis adds to existing work. Later findings may warrant a deliberate revision of the living European paper; this reading does not edit its manuscript.

## Checks to carry into any replication

Resolve the arithmetic in footnote 5 and reconcile Appendix B's sample flow with the Figure A1/A2 captions before importing their totals. Audit source gender coding against Table A1 rather than assuming uniform self-report. Check the independence assumptions behind pooled uncertainty and monthly comparisons. These are replication questions, not findings that invalidate the report.
