How to read this edition

Use measures, attitudes, occupational exposure and observed outcomes are kept distinct. Higher use is not automatically better; exposure can lead to augmentation, displacement or neither. Evidence about productivity is not by itself evidence about who gains in pay, time or job quality.

Published percentages retain their source precision. Missing or inaccessible information is not treated as zero. Multiple publications from one survey share a sample-family identifier, and source counts are not counts of independent studies.

Original empirical research remains central to the programme. The existing European paper supplies our first analysis. New deep dives will follow the landscape and feed reviewed findings back into future editions.
