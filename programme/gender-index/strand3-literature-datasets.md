STRAND 3: LITERATURE AND PUBLIC DATA ON GENDER AND GENERATIVE AI, 2023-2026

Tags: [OPEN] = downloadable today, no application; [REG] = free registration; [REQ] = on request/application; [NA] = not available. "Secondary" = the primary page was blocked (403/paywall: HBS, Springer, GESIS, WEF, UCL) and the figure comes from a summary; treat as unverified.

1. ADOPTION AND USE
- Eurostat isoc_ai_iaiu, 2025 (first wave; table updated 5 Jun 2026). ICT household survey, ages 16-74, 35 countries plus EU and euro-area aggregates. My API pull today, EU27: men 34.91%, women 30.45% used generative AI in the last 3 months; work use 16.87% vs 13.87%; private 28.27% vs 22.76%; formal education 9.35% vs 9.30%. Women lead in MK, MT, EE, SI, LT, HR; widest gap IE (9.32pp). Sex is crossed with age bands and with education. [OPEN] aggregates via API: https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/api/dissemination/statistics/1.0/data/isoc_ai_iaiu (licence not checked); mirrored by EIGE: https://dgs-p.eige.europa.eu/data/information/ta_resdig_dig_intuse__isoc_ai_iaiu. No academic paper using this table was found. Note: February press coverage listed four EU countries where women lead (https://www.euronews.com/2026/02/16/as-ai-use-surges-across-the-eu-who-are-the-countries-and-age-groups-using-it-most); the current table adds Malta, so figures get revised.
- Cranney, Delecourt, Koning (2026), "Global Evidence on Gender Gaps and Generative AI Over Time", HBS WP, https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=66548 (403; secondary: https://poetsandquants.com/2026/06/30/harvard-research-a-22-gender-gap-in-ai-use-that-is-barely-budging/). 76 sources, 100+ countries, ~319,000 respondents: "47.8% for men against 39.3% for women – a relative gap of 22%"; gap "stalled at around 16% since early 2025". Data: could not verify.
- Otis, Cranney, Delecourt, Koning (2024), "Global Evidence on Gender Gaps and Generative AI", https://ideas.repec.org/p/osf/osfxxx/h6a7c.html. "16 studies that surveyed 100,000 individuals across 26 countries"; gap persists "even when the opportunity ... to access AI is equalized". Preprint on OSF; a replication dataset could not be verified.
- Humlum and Vestergaard (2025, PNAS), https://knowledge.uchicago.edu/records/j95qg-jxd71. Denmark, 18,000 workers, 11 occupations, survey linked to registers. "Women are 16 percentage points less likely to have used the tool for work." [REQ] Statistics Denmark only.
- Bick, Blandin, Deming (2024), NBER w32966, https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w32966/w32966.pdf. US Real-Time Population Survey, ages 18-64, Aug and Nov 2024, "more than 10,000 respondents". "genAI adoption is 7.5 percentage points higher for men, while early PC adoption was 6 percentage points higher for women." RPS microdata [REG] at https://www.openicpsr.org/openicpsr/project/158081; whether the genAI waves are included could not be verified (403).
- Aldasoro, Armantier, Doerr, Gambacorta, Oliviero (2024), BIS WP 1197, https://bis.org/publ/work1197.htm. US Survey of Consumer Expectations. "while 50% of men already use gen AI, only 37% of women do"; self-assessed knowledge explains "three-quarters of the gap". Sample size and module microdata: not found.
- Carvajal, Franco, Isaksson (2024), "Will Artificial Intelligence Get in the Way of Achieving Gender Equality?", https://www.socialscienceregistry.org/trials/12452. Norway (NHH), 595 students, 1,134 managers (secondary). When AI is explicitly allowed, over 80 percent of both genders want to use it; under a ban the gap reopens among top women. Data: not found.
- Stephany and Duszynski (2026), arXiv 2601.03880, https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03880. UK DSIT Public Attitudes to Data and AI Tracker, 2023-24, N = 9,172. Frequent personal use "20.9% versus 15.5%"; among young, digitally fluent, high-concern respondents gaps "exceed 45 percentage points". Weighted tables [OPEN], Open Government Licence v3.0; respondent-level data [REQ]: "Raw data and conjoint tables are available upon request", https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/public-attitudes-to-data-and-ai-tracker-survey-wave-4. A wave 5 was not found.
- Henseke (2026), "From Exposure to Adoption: Generative AI in European Workplaces", https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18849. EWCS 2024, 35 countries, about 36,600 workers. Use at work 13.8% men vs 9.7% women; gap "concentrated in the most exposed occupations" (7.5pp in the top exposure quintile). EWCS 2024 microdata [REG] via UK Data Service per Eurofound: https://www.eurofound.europa.eu/en/surveys-and-data/surveys/european-working-conditions-survey/ewcs-2024 (study number not verified).
- Di Pietro (2026), "Exploring gender disparities in AI use among European youth", https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10209-026-01384-x (paywalled; secondary). 27 countries, 2024 youth survey; the gap is small and reverses by task: young women use AI less for entertainment and creative tasks, more for schoolwork. The Eurobarometer number could not be verified; Flash EB 545 (n = 26,189, ages 15-30, https://b2find.eudat.eu/dataset/45ddcf6b-a914-5360-ac9f-0e82f722ba02) does not list AI items in its public description.
- Ochoa, Fernández Melero, Revilla: only a July 2025 ESRA presentation was found ("Exploring differences in ChatGPT adoption and usage in Spain: Contrasting survey and metered data findings", https://www.upf.edu/web/webdataopp/dissemination). OSF deposit: not found.
- Pew (17 Jun 2026), "The gender gap in AI", https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/06/17/the-gender-gap-in-ai/. ATP, 17-23 Feb 2026, n = 5,119. Chatbot use: men 50%, women 47% (39% vs 28% in 2024); for work, "40%" of employed men vs "35%" of employed women. ATP microdata [REG] with a lag, https://www.pewresearch.org/american-trends-panel-datasets/; the latest AI wave posted is W152 (Aug 2024).
- Chatterji et al. (2025), "How People Use ChatGPT", https://www.nber.org/papers/w34255. ChatGPT logs, gender inferred from first names. "Early adopters were disproportionately male but the gender gap has narrowed dramatically." [NA].
- Gambacorta, Jappelli, Oliviero (2025), BIS WP 1298, https://www.bis.org/publ/work1298.pdf. Italy, 5,000 individuals; higher use and larger income association for men (gender numbers not extracted).

2. PATTERNS OF USE
Thin. Eurostat purposes (above) are the only harmonised source; among users, 30.53% of women vs 26.77% of men use it for formal education. Pew 2026 (secondary summary of the same URL): ChatGPT 44% for both, Gemini 29% vs 20%, Copilot 22% vs 13%. Chatterji et al. and Di Pietro report task differences. Intensity by gender: Humlum and Vestergaard only.

3. EXPOSURE OF WOMEN'S WORK
- Gmyrek et al. (2025), ILO WP 140, https://www.ilo.org/node/706766. Highest gradient: "female (4.7%) and male employment (2.4%)" globally; "9.6% female vs 3.5% male in Gradient 4 in HICs". ISCO-08 scores [OPEN]: https://github.com/pgmyrek/2025_GenAI_scores_ISCO08 (licence not checked).
- Massenkoff and McCrory (Anthropic, 5 Mar 2026), https://www.anthropic.com/research/labor-market-impacts. US CPS; top-quartile observed-exposure workers are "16 percentage points more likely to be female". Occupation-level data [OPEN], CC-BY: https://huggingface.co/datasets/Anthropic/EconomicIndex. It carries no gender variable; gender comes from merging with CPS or LFS.
- Kochhar (Pew, 2023), https://www.pewresearch.org/social-trends/2023/07/26/which-u-s-workers-are-more-exposed-to-ai-on-their-jobs/: "A greater share of women (21%) than men (17%)".
- Nurski and Ruer (Bruegel, 2024), EU-LFS, https://www.bruegel.org/working-paper/exposure-generative-artificial-intelligence-european-labour-market: "jobs held by women, highly educated and younger workers are more exposed".
- Albanesi et al. (2025), NBER w33451, https://www.nber.org/papers/w33451: 16 European countries, 2011-2019, "female employment shares increased in occupations more exposed to AI".
- Manning, Aguirre, Muro, Methkupally (Brookings, Jan 2026), https://www.brookings.edu/articles/measuring-us-workers-capacity-to-adapt-to-ai-driven-job-displacement: 6.1 million highly exposed, low-adaptability workers; "Of these workers, 86% are women."
- IMF (Cazzaniga et al., 2024), Eloundou et al., Felten et al., Webb: gender-specific figures could not be verified this session.

(a) THICK AND THIN
Thick: adoption (a meta-synthesis already exists); occupational exposure. Moderate: attitudes. Thin: patterns and intensity of use; workplace conditions outside Denmark; outcomes and returns by gender (one preprint study, one student/manager study); representation (proprietary LinkedIn data). Absent: any recurring, multi-dimensional, reproducible measure; any academic use of Eurostat's 2025 table; observed (Anthropic) exposure mapped to European women's employment.

(b) MOST VALUABLE DATASETS
For a recurring index: (1) Eurostat isoc_ai_iaiu: annual, official, 35 countries, sex by age and education, four purposes. (2) ILO ISCO-08 scores joined to Eurostat LFS employment by sex and occupation: exposure, refreshable yearly (LFS table code not checked). (3) Anthropic Economic Index: CC-BY, re-released several times a year, observed rather than theoretical exposure. (4) Pew ATP: repeated US items with a 2024-2026 trend. (5) SHED: annual open US microdata; DSIT tracker tables as the UK aggregate alternative.
For a standalone paper: (1) EWCS 2024 microdata: AI use alongside working conditions in 35 countries. (2) Eurobarometer 101.4 (ZA8844): workplace AI experience and attitudes, apparently unused for gender. (3) SHED 2025: AI at work linked to household finances. (4) Pew ATP W152 and later. (5) Eurostat cell-level table: country by sex, age, education, purpose.

(c) CANDIDATE PAPERS
1. Where and for whom does the gap reverse? Women lead in six of 35 countries and in educational use. Explain cross-country and cohort variation in the gap. Data: Eurostat isoc_ai_iaiu. Matters because syntheses pool non-comparable surveys; this is the first harmonised official source.
2. The exposure-use mismatch. Women hold the most exposed jobs (ILO, Anthropic) yet use AI less, most of all in exposed occupations (Henseke). Construct an exposure-adjusted gap by country using observed exposure. Data: Anthropic release plus ILO scores plus LFS plus EWCS. Matters for who bears displacement risk without the productivity offset.
3. Do training, encouragement and permission close the gap outside Denmark? Humlum and Vestergaard's result rests on non-shareable register data. Data: EWCS 2024 or Eurobarometer 101.4, SHED for the US. Matters because it is the actionable employer lever. Check the relevant variables exist before committing; I did not inspect the questionnaires.

(d) STRONGEST COMPARATOR
Cranney, Delecourt and Koning (2026), with Pew's June 2026 report as the US benchmark. Differ by: covering exposure, conditions and outcomes as well as use; relying on harmonised official statistics rather than pooled heterogeneous surveys; publishing code and data on a fixed release cycle; reporting cells (age, education, purpose) where the gap reverses; and not treating men's adoption rate as the norm, the point pressed by Stephany and Duszynski. EIGE already mirrors the Eurostat table, so an institutional competitor is plausible.

CAVEATS FOR THE INDEX DESIGN
Question wording differs across sources (Eurostat: used tools "to create content" in the last three months; EWCS: AI at work; Pew: chatbots), so levels are not comparable across datasets. Gender is binary sex in all official sources and name-inferred in platform logs. Bick et al.'s gap is widely reported as 9pp in press summaries; the working paper text I read says 7.5pp.
