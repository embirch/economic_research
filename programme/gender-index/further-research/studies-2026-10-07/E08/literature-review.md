## Novelty conclusion

E08 contributes a descriptive employee comparison using reported GenAI participation and unpaid free-time job-work frequency, while making population restriction and composition targets explicit. Neither AI/work-life balance nor gender differences in working time are new topics. The latest inaccessible Jiang full text remains a material limit on any stronger novelty claim. The paper therefore makes no first-ever claim and does not infer that the unpaid work occurred because of AI.

## Relevant HBS foundation, assessed internally

The foundational [Cranney, Delecourt and Koning report, May 2026](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/25-023_be8fb517-3dd5-40aa-97f9-4e42e1c8e6ff.pdf) was fully read in the programme's 1 October record at `../../../landscape-v1/coordination/HBS-READING.md`. This execution reuses that reading and does not claim a fresh pooled replication.

The relevant finding is a gender participation gap across heterogeneous adoption evidence, with occupational context and mechanisms organized in Table 2. Its interpretation connects unequal participation to possible unequal access to benefits. That connection requires assumptions about the task, adoption's effects and who receives time or income gains. E08 does not test the review's pooled adoption result, and no pooled world rate is used here.

E08 extends that interpretation by treating reported participation and a work burden as separate measured outcomes. Higher participation need not imply a lower burden in an observed cross-section. Its raw gender interaction is also sensitive to the common job mix, consistent with the foundation's need to examine context, while not establishing an occupational mechanism. The results qualify a welfare reading of participation alone; they do not show that widening adoption reduces welfare or that women's lower adoption protects them.

The foundation's proposed barriers such as familiarity, usefulness, institutional support, legitimacy and trust remain hypotheses outside E08's measures. Within broad job strata, unobserved task and selection differences persist. The complete source lineage, measure definitions and limitations therefore stay attached to claims. These internal foundation assessments are not public findings or manuscript headings.
