### (a) Verdict
Nothing equivalent exists. No index in this strand combines sex-disaggregated AI access, use, exposure and outcomes; none is recurring and reproducible from public data. The established gender indices contain no AI indicator. AI appears either as a LinkedIn sidebar (WEF, OECD.AI) or as recycled headline statistics (UN Women, UNESCO).

### (b) Three closest, and how the proposal differs
1. **Eurostat isoc_ai_iaiu, mirrored by EIGE.** Official, open, by sex, annual. It covers use only, the EU only, and one wave so far. The proposal would add exposure and outcomes, non-EU sources and a harmonised gap metric.
2. **WEF Global Gender Gap Report AI sections and the WEF/LinkedIn white paper.** Recurring and global, but talent and skills only, on proprietary LinkedIn data, outside the index itself. The proposal would be open, reproducible and centred on use.
3. **Women in AI Statistics 2026.** Annual, public, with a CSV, but it is a list of others' figures with no harmonisation, definitions or series. The proposal would be an instrument with fixed constructs.

Runners-up: the Gender Snapshot 2026 AI spotlight and the WiD Index.

### (c) Methodological lessons
- **Prefer a dashboard of parity ratios to a weighted composite.** ITU's female/male ratio with a 0.98–1.02 parity band is transparent. EIGE needed AHP expert weights and dropped its correcting coefficient because it was "complex and in some cases had a disproportionate effect on results".
- **Report relative and absolute gaps together.** UN Women says "around 20 per cent less likely"; Eurostat shows 34.91% vs 30.45%, a 4.5-point gap and a ratio of 0.87 (my calculation). The two framings read very differently.
- **Disaggregate by purpose.** On Eurostat the EU gap sits in work and private use and is near zero for formal education.
- **Version the method and back-cast.** EIGE's 2025 redesign set "a new baseline" that is "no longer comparable" with earlier scores, and it recalculated past values.
- **Avoid silent reweighting for missing data.** The WiD Index rescales weights when a pillar is missing, so country scores rest on different baskets. SIGI instead publishes scores for 140 of 179 countries. Publishing coverage and leaving gaps blank is the safer model.
- **Do not build on platform data alone.** UNESCO's study is summarised as finding gender-disaggregated AI data "drawn heavily from LinkedIn and Global North sources"; interface infers gender from first names.
- **Keep survey definitions fixed.** Eurostat's "last 3 months" and Pew's chatbot-use question are not interchangeable; present them side by side, not pooled.
