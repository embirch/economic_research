### (a) Coverage by dimension
- **Adoption** is well covered and the only dimension with open, recurring data: Eurostat (annual, EU) and OpenAI Signals (monthly, global). Pew and the RPS recur, but Pew releases no microdata here and the RPS tracker has no gender series.
- **Patterns of use** has one open source, Signals topic × gender. Anthropic's gender result is a single paragraph with no released data.
- **Exposure of work** is strong analytically (ILO 2023/2025/2026, IMF, PwC, OECD) but not a maintained open series by sex.
- **Builders** recur annually (OECD.AI, WEF/LinkedIn, AI Index), all from proprietary LinkedIn data. The AI Index has dropped its diversity chapter.
- **Workplace conditions and training** are thin: Women in the Workplace and Coursera are annual and closed; Randstad is one-off.
- **Attitudes and trust** recur (Pew, DSIT, Reuters) but mostly without open gender tables.
- **Outcomes and returns** are empty. No recurring source reports wage, productivity or promotion returns to AI by gender.

### (b) Best candidate series per dimension
- **Adoption:** Eurostat isoc_ai_iaiu (annual, EU countries, by sex and purpose); Pew (US, 2024 and 2026); the Cranney et al. synthesis for global context.
- **Patterns of use:** OpenAI Signals gender × topic × month (CC BY 4.0).
- **Exposure:** ILO index applied to labour force survey employment by sex, recomputed each year.
- **Builders:** OECD.AI proportion of AI talent by gender (annual; 2016–2024).
- **Training:** Coursera women's share of GenAI enrolments (annual).
- **Workplace:** Women in the Workplace (annual, US).
- **Attitudes:** Pew, plus DSIT (UK).
- **Outcomes:** none.

### (c) Comparability traps
- **Recall window:** Pew "ever use" (50/47), Eurostat "last 3 months" (34.9/30.5), NY Fed "previous 12 months" (50/37) and Oliver Wyman "at least once a week" (59/51) are not the same quantity.
- **Messages versus people:** Signals reports shares of messages, so intensity is mixed with headcount. Similarweb counts visits; JPMorgan counts firms.
- **Gender measure:** Signals uses names above 95% prevalence, which drops ambiguous names and non-Western naming unevenly. LinkedIn infers gender with a coverage threshold. Surveys are self-report.
- **Population:** household heads (NY Fed), workers (Oliver Wyman, RPS work use), ages 16–74 (Eurostat), one product's users (Anthropic at 12% women, Signals).
- **Relative versus absolute gaps:** "22%" is relative; the underlying gap is 8.5 points.

### (d) Is there already a de facto index?
Partly. The Cranney, Delecourt and Koning synthesis is the de facto global measure of the **adoption** gap, but it covers adoption only, is a working paper rather than a scheduled release, and I found no public dataset. OpenAI Signals is the only open, recurring, gender-disaggregated instrument, limited to one product, messages and name inference. Nothing combines adoption, use, exposure, workplace, outcomes, attitudes and builders on a regular open schedule.
