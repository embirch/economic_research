# Gender & AI research programme

Current project record, approved by Emily Birch on 30 September 2026. Read this before historical handovers, old briefs or agent memories. [Decisions](programme/DECISIONS.md) record what changed; [file map](programme/FILE-MAP.md) identifies the authoritative outputs.

## Purpose

Understand gender differences in adoption, use and experience of generative AI, and investigate what these differences may mean for society and the economy. Produce transparent, reproducible empirical research in Emily's own voice, accessible to interested readers and defensible to specialist reviewers.

The intended programme combines a maintained Gender & AI Index or evidence explorer with approximately three substantial research papers. The index's final form, coverage and paper choices depend on verified data and a distinct contribution. No composite score, worldwide ranking, causal pathway or release schedule is committed. The fellowship application is a motivation, not a constraint that every study use Anthropic data or imitate its prose.

## Current scope

- Participation; purposes and patterns of use; experience, barriers and workplace opportunity. Occupational exposure provides context where its interpretation is clear. Economic returns remain an evidence question, not an assumed consequence of use.
- Anthropic research is supplementary intellectual and empirical material. Select sources by fitness for the question, including official surveys, other platforms, experiments and qualitative studies.
- Preserve distinctions between generative AI and broader AI, people and messages, reported sex/gender and inferred proxies, participation and benefits, exposure and realised outcomes.
- Interpret female advantages and null findings as carefully as male advantages. More use is not automatically better, and men's use is not a normative target.
