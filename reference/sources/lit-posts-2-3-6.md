**Where Post 3 sits.** The closest prior work is again Anthropic's: the 2026 observed-exposure paper already compares Eloundou's task-level β with realised Claude usage (68% of usage on β=1 tasks; 97% of observed tasks in feasible categories; observed exposure predicts BLS projections while Eloundou alone does not) — so "predicted vs observed" at the task level is published, with Tomlinson 2025 as the Copilot analogue and Yin 2026 as the platform-selection critique. The measure-agreement literature (Gimbel 2026; Frank 2023; Henseke 2025) and the Frey–Osborne retrospectives (Coelli & Borland 2019; Georgieff & Milanez 2021; Arntz 2016) each exist separately. Nobody has joined them: treating Eloundou/Felten/Webb as *forecasts* and scoring them with forecast-evaluation tools — calibration of β against observed penetration, rank vs level accuracy, and a decomposition of misses into capability error (β=0 tasks that are used) versus diffusion error (β=1 tasks never used, e.g. drug-refill authorisation) — and then asking whether the error structure matches the previous generation's. The specific historical lesson is that F&O ranked occupations tolerably (Georgieff & Milanez) but the level forecast failed because diffusion, complementarity and unit-of-analysis were ignored; LLM measures fixed the unit of analysis (task-level) but still forecast capability, not diffusion, and Henseke 2025 shows their level statements are rater-dependent. The unexplained "Interestingly" in the Anthropic paper — why the Eloundou measure alone does not track BLS projections — is the natural hook. Must engage Yin 2026 (platform selection) and the BLS evaluation literature, and must distinguish forecasting *exposure* from forecasting *employment* (Humlum vs Brynjolfsson).

**Where Post 6 sits.** The convergence claim and its first correction are Anthropic's own (Jan 2026 β≈0.77 OLS vs 0.89 2SLS and "2–5 years"; Jun 2026 revision to "5–9 years" with a Gini path 0.37→0.31→0.29 for states but 0.48→0.46→0.50 for countries). The econometric critique is old and settled but has never been applied to this setting: Quah 1993 and Friedman 1992 on Galton's fallacy, Cannon & Duck 2000 on transitory noise biasing toward convergence, Hauk & Wacziarg 2009 on estimators overstating convergence speed under measurement error, Young et al. 2008 on β without σ in US sub-national data, and Kane & Staiger 2002 on small units dominating the extremes of noisy rankings. The diffusion baselines (Griliches 1957; Comin & Hobijn 2010; Comin & Mestieri 2018; Skinner & Staiger 2005; Doms 2005; Andrés et al. 2010) say that for previous general-purpose technologies level gaps narrowed slowly, intensity gaps often widened, and state/metro rank orderings barely moved. The gap: no one has (i) simulated the sampling-noise null for state AUI given one-week windows, ≥15-conversation/≥5-account privacy cells and state sample sizes (draw each state's conversations multinomially from a fixed national distribution, recompute β and Gini), (ii) tested σ-convergence and rank persistence against that null, (iii) checked whether the Aug→Nov→Feb slowdown is what attenuation predicts as samples grow or as the same states are re-measured, or (iv) reconciled state convergence with country non-convergence (Microsoft, World Bank) and with Comin–Mestieri's intensive-margin divergence. The Utah abuse flag and the Sept 2025 report's own admission that "limited observations for smaller countries and states" motivate future stratified sampling are the on-record hooks.
