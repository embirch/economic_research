## 2. Foundational papers (16)

★ = must read first.

1. ★ **Brundage, Avin, Clark, Toner, Eckersley et al. (2018), "The Malicious Use of AI: Forecasting, Prevention, and Mitigation"** — https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07228 — Founding taxonomy of digital/physical/political attack surfaces; Jack Clark is an author, so it is the genealogical root of the TAI pillar.
2. ★ **Garfinkel & Dafoe (2019), "How does the offense-defense balance scale?", *J. Strategic Studies* 42(6):736–763** — https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/01402390.2019.1631810 — Formalises O-D balance via contest success functions; "offensive-then-defensive scaling." The theoretical spine of the fellowship's stated question.
3. ★ **Shevlane & Dafoe (2020), "The Offense-Defense Balance of Scientific Knowledge: Does Publishing AI Research Reduce Misuse?", AIES** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00463 — When disclosure helps defenders more than attackers; imports the vulnerability-disclosure debate into AI.
4. ★ **Shevlane, Farquhar, Garfinkel, Phuong, Whittlestone, Anderljung et al. (2023), "Model evaluation for extreme risks"** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15324 — Dangerous-capability + propensity evaluations; the conceptual basis for RSP-style thresholds.
5. ★ **Anderljung, Barnhart, Korinek, Leung, O'Keefe, Whittlestone et al. (2023), "Frontier AI Regulation"** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03718 — Standard-setting, registration/reporting, compliance. The regulatory counterpart to the RSP.
6. ★ **Bernardi, Mukobi, Greaves, Heim, Anderljung (2024), "Societal Adaptation to Advanced AI"** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.10295 — Avoid / defend / remedy framework; the canonical citation for "resilience" as a complement to prevention. Directly matches the fellowship's "societal resilience mechanisms."
7. **Gandhi, Cihon, Larter, Anselmetti (2025), "Societal Capacity Assessment Framework"** (TAIG @ ICML 2025) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22742 — Indicators-based vulnerability / coping capacity / adaptive capacity measurement at country level. The most operational resilience-measurement proposal available, and an obvious template for a fellowship project.
8. ★ **Mouton, Lucas, Guest (2024), "The Operational Risks of AI in Large-Scale Biological Attacks: Results of a Red-Team Study", RAND** — https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA2977-2.html — No statistically significant uplift from then-current LLMs. The main empirical counterweight to alarm; know its limits (2023-era models, plan viability as the outcome).
9. ★ **Gopal, Helm-Burger, Justen, Soice, Esvelt et al. (2023), "Will releasing the weights of future LLMs grant widespread access to pandemic agents?"** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.18233 — "Spicy" fine-tune of Llama-2-70B gave participants nearly all information needed to obtain 1918 influenza. The core open-weights argument.
10. **Sandbrink (2023), "AI and biological misuse: differentiating risks of language models and biological design tools"** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13952 — LLMs lower barriers; BDTs raise the ceiling. Keeps you from conflating the two threat models.
11. **Götting, Medeiros, Sanders, Li et al. (2025), "Virology Capabilities Test (VCT)"** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16137 — 322 multimodal questions; o3 at 43.8% vs expert virologists' 22.1% in their own specialties. The headline "AI beats experts" bio result.
12. ★ **Fang, Bindu, Gupta, Kang (2024), "LLM Agents can Autonomously Exploit One-day Vulnerabilities"** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.08144 — GPT-4 exploited 87% of 15 CVEs given the description; 0% for everything else. The first widely cited offense datapoint.
13. **Zhang, Perry, Dulepet, Ji, Menders et al. (2024), "Cybench"** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08926 **[URL UNVERIFIED — confirm the arXiv ID; the benchmark itself is verified via https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/cybench]** — 40 CTF tasks from recent competitions; the standard agentic cyber benchmark alongside CyberSecEval (https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04724) and CVE-Bench (https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17332).
14. ★ **Lohn & Jackson (2022), "Will AI Make Cyber Swords or Shields?", CSET** — https://cset.georgetown.edu/wp-content/uploads/Will-AI-Make-Cyber-Swords-or-Shields-A-few-mathematical-models-of-technological-progress.pdf (arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.13825) — Models phishing, vulnerability discovery, and patch/exploit racing; finds exploit-writing automation helps attackers more than patch-writing, but faster patch *deployment* may dominate both. The best formal bridge between benchmark results and policy.
15. **Phuong et al. (2024), "Evaluating Frontier Models for Dangerous Capabilities", DeepMind** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13793 — Cross-lab comparator to Anthropic's Frontier Red Team methodology.
16. **Beraja, Kao, Yang, Yuchtman (2023), "AI-tocracy", *QJE* 138(3):1349–1402** — https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/138/3/1349/7076890 (NBER w29466: https://www.nber.org/papers/w29466) — Autocratic demand for surveillance AI drives innovation, which drives exports and further repression. ★ for the surveillance sub-theme, and the strongest *economics* paper in this space — highly relevant given the track is Economics & Policy.

**Also cite selectively:** Feldstein, "The Global Expansion of AI Surveillance" (Carnegie, Sep 2019, AIGS Index for 176 countries) — https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2019/09/the-global-expansion-of-ai-surveillance ; Hendrycks, Mazeika, Woodside, "An Overview of Catastrophic AI Risks" — https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12001 ; Hendrycks, Schmidt, Wang, "Superintelligence Strategy" (MAIM) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05628 ; Kolt, Anderljung, Barnhart, Esvelt, Heim et al., "Responsible Reporting for Frontier AI Development" — https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.02675 ; Sastry, Heim, Belfield, Anderljung, Brundage, Egan et al., "Computing Power and the Governance of AI" — https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08797 ; Goldstein, Sastry, Musser, DiResta, Gentzel, Sedova, "Generative Language Models and Automated Influence Operations" — https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04246 ; Li et al., "WMDP Benchmark" — https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03218 ; Corsi, Kilian, Mallah, "Considerations Influencing Offense-Defense Dynamics From AI" — https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04029 ; **International AI Safety Report 2026** (Bengio chair, 91+ co-authors, Feb 2026) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21012 plus the 2025 key updates https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.13653 and https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.19863.

## 4. Key things to know

**Vocabulary (be able to define each in one sentence, with a citation).**
1. **Offense–defense balance** — relative cost of attack vs defence at a given investment level; Garfinkel & Dafoe show it can flip with scale ("OD-scaling").
2. **Uplift** — the marginal capability a model gives an actor *over their counterfactual baseline* (usually internet access). The entire bio debate turns on how you define the baseline and the outcome measure.
3. **Dual-use** — same capability serves benign and harmful ends; TAI's framing is that dual-use is *inherent*, not an edge case.
4. **Dual-use observability** — whether you can measure harmful deployment at all. TAI's distinctive contribution: the measurement problem, not just the risk.
5. **ASL levels / capability thresholds** — Anthropic's tiered safety levels tied to capability triggers (ASL-3 live since May 2025).
6. **Differential technology development / differential access** — deliberately advancing defensive applications first; IAPS's core recommendation and Glasswing's explicit theory.
7. **Defensive acceleration (d/acc)** — Buterin-associated framing: accelerate defence rather than pause offence.
8. **Societal adaptation / resilience** — reducing harm at a *given* capability level (avoid/defend/remedy, Bernardi et al.).
9. **Strategic warning** — Anthropic's own term for early-warning capability trend reporting before thresholds are crossed.
10. **Risk pricing** — using markets (insurance, catastrophe bonds, prediction markets, security bug bounties) to generate forward-looking risk estimates; the fellowship explicitly wants "market driven mechanisms."
11. **Attack surface / N-day vs 0-day** — Anthropic's June 2026 result is that the *N-day* window (patch published → working exploit) collapsed to hours.
12. **Structured access** — capability release gated by verified user and use case; Mythos/Glasswing is the live experiment.
13. **KYC for compute** — compute-provider customer verification as a governance chokepoint (Sastry/Heim et al.).
14. **MAIM (Mutual Assured AI Malfunction)** — Hendrycks/Schmidt/Wang deterrence framing; know it well enough to critique it.
15. **Agentic scaffolding** — the tooling wrapped around a model; Anthropic's Navigator finds this, not raw skill, separates dangerous from ordinary actors.

**Key tensions and open questions.**

1. **Does AI favour offense or defense in cyber?** *Offense:* Fang et al. (87% one-day exploitation); Anthropic's N-days post (exploit in 31 minutes for ~$2,200); GTG-1002 (80–90% autonomous); Withers on Scaling Laws. *Defense:* Lohn & Jackson (faster patch *deployment* may dominate); Glasswing's 10,000+ flaws found by defenders; Garfinkel & Dafoe's OD-scaling implies defence wins at high investment. *Strong answer:* the balance is not a scalar — decompose by phase (discovery, weaponisation, deployment, persistence) and by defender class; the AI advantage is concentrated in *discovery and weaponisation*, where offense is favoured, while patch-deployment lag is an organisational, not technical, bottleneck. That means resource-poor defenders lose even if the aggregate balance shifts defensive.
2. **Does AI favour offense or defense in bio?** *Offense:* Gopal et al. (open weights); VCT (models above expert virologists); Anthropic's own Claude 4 uplift trial. *Defense/sceptical:* RAND's null result; Epoch's critique that benchmarks don't measure the binding constraint (tacit lab skill, materials acquisition); Anthropic's own null wet-lab pilot (n=8). *Strong answer:* bio and cyber have opposite structures — cyber's bottleneck is cognitive (so AI bites), bio's is physical and logistical (so AI bites less, for now), and this justifies asymmetric policy (DNA synthesis screening rather than model restriction).
3. **Is "uplift" measurable, and does it matter?** RAND vs Anthropic disagree partly because they measure different outcomes. Note that Anthropic is essentially the only lab running text-based uplift trials on frontier models, and that its Mythos assessment reportedly reached "CB-1 force-multiplier" but not the CB-2 threshold **[UNVERIFIED — reported via an EA Forum survey of mid-2026 bio-uplift research, https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/S6ydgTdTr8sXkfs9x/the-state-of-bio-uplift-research-in-mid-2026 — confirm against Anthropic's own model card before citing]**. Best applicant move: propose a *design* fix (pre-registered outcomes, real-world end-to-end task completion, adversarial elicitation) rather than picking a side.
4. **Open weights vs closed.** Gopal et al. vs the diffusion/defence-access argument; Mythos gating is the strongest current closed-side data point, and also the strongest illustration of the equity objection (defenders who can pay get the model; everyone else waits 6–12 months for proliferation).
5. **Unilateral lab commitments vs regulation.** RSP v3 says the consensus-building theory "did not play out." Positions: GovAI (honest downgrade, net acceptable); Zvi/critics (a commitment to document replaced a commitment to stop). Evidence on the other side: SB 53 and the EU GPAI Code show regulation *did* arrive, just slowly and thinly.
6. **Surveillance/repression as the quiet risk vs catastrophic misuse.** TAI's agenda names surveillance explicitly, and Anthropic literally lost federal contracts over refusing mass domestic surveillance — yet almost all Frontier Red Team output is cyber and bio. This gap is the single most defensible reason to pitch a surveillance project.
7. **Strategic warning: who gets told, and when?** Kolt et al. on responsible reporting; FMF's information-sharing agreement (member firms only, signed March 2025); the espionage disclosure showed the lab as first-responder. Open question: does company-to-state disclosure crowd out state capacity, or build it?
8. **Resilience/adaptation vs prevention.** Bernardi et al. and the SCAF argue for capacity measurement; critics say adaptation is a licence to proceed. TAI clearly leans adaptation ("closing the gap between fast offense and slow defence").
9. **Measuring dual-use in deployment.** Anthropic can see its own logs (832 accounts, 13,873 actions) but nobody can see the aggregate. Should there be a cross-lab, privacy-preserving misuse telemetry standard? Nobody has a good answer — good territory.
10. **State capacity vs company capacity in crisis.** In Nov 2025 a private company detected, attributed, and disrupted a state-sponsored campaign. That is an institutional anomaly. What does company-to-state and state-to-state escalation infrastructure look like — and who is on the other end of the phone?
11. **US–China dynamics.** AI-tocracy (surveillance demand drives innovation and export), MAIM (deterrence), export controls, GTG-1002. Jim Baker's brief is explicitly US institutions and China competition.
12. **Can regulation ever close the timescale gap?** TAI's own framing (months vs years) suggests not by legislation alone — hence insurance, standards, procurement, liability, and market mechanisms. This is where an economist has comparative advantage.

## 7. Empirical project ideas (4 months, public data)

1. **An offense–defense index from patch-to-exploit timing.** Data: NVD/CVE + CISA KEV + ExploitDB + vendor advisories, plus Anthropic's N-days and Firefox posts as ground truth. Method: survival analysis of time-to-public-exploit and time-to-KEV-listing, before/after frontier model releases, with a difference-in-differences using vulnerability classes that are more vs less amenable to LLM exploitation. TAI question: is the offense-defense balance actually shifting, or only the observable exploit *tempo*?
2. **Replicating and extending the ATT&CK Navigator externally.** Data: Anthropic's published Navigator + OpenAI/Google/Meta threat reports + MITRE ATT&CK. Method: build a comparable ARiES-style scoring of *cross-lab* published misuse cases; test whether the 33%→56% medium-risk shift replicates outside Anthropic's user base. TAI question: dual-use observability — can misuse be measured without privileged log access?
3. **Risk pricing via cyber insurance.** Data: state DOI rate filings (California, New York), Lloyd's market reports, catastrophe-bond spreads, public 10-K risk-factor disclosures. Method: text-analyse filings for AI-related exclusions/loadings; test whether premiums or exclusions moved after Nov 2025 and Jun 2026. TAI question: does a market that prices tail risk see AI-enabled attack risk before regulators do?
4. **Regulatory lag as a measurable quantity.** Data: capability milestone dates (Epoch, model cards, red.anthropic.com) vs statutory/regulatory dates (SB 53, EU AI Act, state AI bills via NCSL, executive actions). Method: construct a lag distribution per capability domain; model what determines shorter lags. TAI question: quantifies Anthropic's own "months vs years" claim, which is currently asserted without data.
5. **AI surveillance procurement from public tenders.** Data: TED (EU), USASpending/SAM.gov, UK Contracts Finder, Open Contracting Partnership data. Method: keyword + classifier extraction of facial recognition / video analytics / "smart city" contracts; build a price-per-camera or price-per-capability time series to test whether AI made surveillance *cheaper*, *more effective*, or both. Cross-validate against Feldstein's AIGS index and Freedom on the Net scores. TAI question: this is literally the agenda's stated surveillance question, and almost nobody is answering it with price data.
6. **Do Anthropic's own threat-intel reports show escalating autonomy?** Data: all Anthropic threat reports (Mar 2025, Aug 2025, Nov 2025, Jun 2026) plus OpenAI and Google TAG equivalents. Method: code every documented case for degree of autonomy, attack phase, actor sophistication, and scaffolding used; test whether autonomy rises faster than actor sophistication. TAI question: is the binding constraint the model or the scaffolding?
7. **Defender diffusion lag.** Data: Glasswing partner list (~150 orgs, 15+ countries) vs critical-infrastructure sector inventories; CISA KEV remediation deadlines vs actual patch rates from Shodan/Censys-style scan data. Method: measure how long defensive capability takes to reach the median defender vs the frontier defender. TAI question: the equity objection to structured access — who is left behind, and for how long?
8. **A country-level resilience index.** Data: operationalise SCAF (arXiv 2509.22742) using ITU cybersecurity index, WHO IHR/JEE biosecurity scores, Freedom House, World Bank governance indicators. Method: build the index, then validate against observed AI-incident outcomes in the AI Incident Database and OECD AIM. TAI question: "societal resilience mechanisms" — makes resilience a number you can regress on.
9. **Bug bounty prices as a capability signal.** Data: HackerOne/Bugcrowd public disclosure feeds, Zerodium-style broker price lists, Pwn2Own payouts. Method: test whether bounty prices for LLM-tractable bug classes fell relative to hard classes after mid-2025. TAI question: a market-based, real-time measure of exploit-supply — closest thing available to a live offense-defense price signal.
10. **AI-related incident reporting under SB 53.** Data: California AG critical-safety-incident reports (first full year of the regime), plus company transparency reports. Method: compare what is reported to what appears in AIID/OECD AIM; measure disclosure completeness. TAI question: does mandatory reporting actually generate strategic warning, or duplicate what labs publish voluntarily?
