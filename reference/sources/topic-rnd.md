## 2. Foundational papers (the ones the community actually cites)

**MUST READ FIRST** = ★

1. ★ **I.J. Good (1965), "Speculations Concerning the First Ultraintelligent Machine,"** Advances in Computers 6. Origin of the "intelligence explosion" idea; quoted by CSET, Forethought and TAI. **[canonical]** e.g. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0065245808604180
2. **Yudkowsky (2013), "Intelligence Explosion Microeconomics,"** MIRI. First attempt to make "returns on cognitive reinvestment" a quantitative question. https://intelligence.org/files/IEM.pdf **[canonical]**. Companion: **Hanson–Yudkowsky AI-Foom Debate** (2008-13) https://intelligence.org/ai-foom-debate/ — the origin of the local-vs-distributed takeoff disagreement.
3. **Bostrom (2014), *Superintelligence*,** OUP, ch. 4 "The kinetics of an intelligence explosion" (recalcitrance vs optimization power; fast/moderate/slow takeoff). **[canonical]**
4. ★ **Christiano (2018), "Takeoff speeds,"** The Sideways View. Argues for continuous but fast takeoff (a 4-year GDP doubling before a 1-year doubling); the reference point for "continuous vs discontinuous." https://sideways-view.com/2018/02/24/takeoff-speeds/ **[canonical]**
5. ★ **Davidson (2023), "What a compute-centric framework says about takeoff speeds,"** Open Philanthropy. The semi-endogenous growth model behind most modern takeoff estimates; introduces the r parameter (returns to software R&D). https://www.openphilanthropy.org/research/what-a-compute-centric-framework-says-about-takeoff-speeds/ **[canonical]**
6. ★ **Eth & Davidson (2025), "Will AI R&D automation cause a software intelligence explosion?"** Forethought, March 2025. https://www.forethought.org/research/will-ai-r-and-d-automation-cause-a-software-intelligence-explosion **[search-verified]**. Argues r>1 is plausible so a software-only explosion could occur even with fixed compute. Sequel: **Davidson & Houlden (Aug 2025), "How quick and big would a software intelligence explosion be?"** https://www.forethought.org/research/how-quick-and-big-would-a-software-intelligence-explosion-be **[search-verified]**; and the interactive **Speed-up calculator** https://newsletter.forethought.org/p/speed-up-calculator-how-much-will
7. **Whitfill & Wu (2025), "Will Compute Bottlenecks Prevent an Intelligence Explosion?"** arXiv 2507.23181. Builds a 2014-2024 panel of four labs; estimates elasticity of substitution between research compute and cognitive labor; results diverge by specification (substitutes vs complements). Best empirical entry into the bottleneck debate. https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.23181 **[search-verified]**
8. **Ord (2026), "The Dynamics of Intelligence Explosions,"** Forethought / arXiv 2608.14426. Shows generation time is the neglected parameter; "singular" growth requires generation time -> 0; identifies super-exponential-but-non-singular regimes. https://newsletter.forethought.org/p/the-dynamics-of-intelligence-explosions **[search-verified]**
9. ★ **Erdil & Besiroglu (2023), "Explosive growth from AI automation: A review of the arguments,"** Epoch / arXiv 2309.11690. The balanced survey of whether AI could raise growth by ~10x. https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11690 **[search-verified]**
10. ★ **Aghion, Jones & Jones (2017/2019), "Artificial Intelligence and Economic Growth,"** NBER w23928 / in *The Economics of AI: An Agenda*. Task-based growth model; Baumol "the growth rate is set by what is essential but hard to improve." https://www.nber.org/papers/w23928 **[search-verified]**
11. **Jones (2024), "The AI Dilemma: Growth versus Existential Risk,"** AER: Insights 6(4). How much x-risk a society should accept for faster growth; the economist's framing of the intervention-point question. https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aeri.20230570 **[search-verified]**
12. **Trammell & Korinek (2023), "Economic Growth under Transformative AI,"** NBER w31815 (survey of growth theory with AI). **[canonical]** https://www.nber.org/papers/w31815 ; **Korinek & Suh (2024), "Scenarios for the Transition to AGI,"** NBER w32255 **[canonical]** https://www.nber.org/papers/w32255
13. ★ **Bloom, Jones, Van Reenen & Webb (2020), "Are Ideas Getting Harder to Find?"** AER 110(4). Research productivity falling ~5%/yr; the empirical basis for "diminishing returns" terms in every takeoff model. **[canonical]** https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.20180338
14. **Acemoglu (2024), "The Simple Macroeconomics of AI,"** NBER w32487 — the skeptical benchmark (~0.7% TFP over 10 years). **[canonical]** https://www.nber.org/papers/w32487
15. ★ **Kwa et al. / METR (2025), "Measuring AI Ability to Complete Long Tasks,"** arXiv 2503.14499 — 50% task horizon doubling every ~7 months (2019-25). https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14499 **[search-verified]**. Update: **METR "Time Horizon 1.1"** (29 Jan 2026): ~3.5-month doubling for 2024-25 models, 228 tasks. https://metr.org/blog/2026-1-29-time-horizon-1-1/ **[search-verified]**
16. ★ **Becker et al. / METR (2025), "Measuring the Impact of Early-2025 AI on Experienced Open-Source Developer Productivity,"** arXiv 2507.09089 — RCT, 16 developers, +19% completion time (CI +2% to +39%) despite believing they were 20% faster. https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/ **[search-verified]**. Follow-ups: design change (24 Feb 2026) https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/ (returning-developer subset now ~18% speedup, CI -38% to +9%, with selection problems), and self-report survey (11 May 2026) https://metr.org/blog/2026-05-11-ai-usage-survey/ **[search-verified]**
17. **Wijk et al. / METR (2024), "RE-Bench,"** arXiv 2411.15114 **[canonical]**; **Chan et al. / OpenAI (2024), "MLE-bench,"** arXiv 2410.07095 **[canonical]**; **Starace et al. / OpenAI (2025), "PaperBench,"** arXiv 2504.01848 **[canonical]** — the three AI-R&D-specific benchmarks cited by RSPs and the International AI Safety Report.
18. **Chan, Padarath, Kwon, Greaves & Anderljung (2026), "Measuring AI R&D Automation,"** arXiv 2603.03992 (GovAI-affiliated). Proposes tracking capital share devoted to automation, researcher time allocation, subversion incidents, oversight capacity. https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03992 **[fetched]**
19. **Owen / Epoch (2024), "Interviewing AI researchers on automation of AI R&D."** Engineering seen as more automatable than ideation; timelines from years to centuries. https://epoch.ai/blog/interviewing-ai-researchers-on-automation-of-ai-rnd **[search-verified]**
20. **Kirgis, Kapoor et al. (2026), "Can AI agents conduct open-ended AI research?"** arXiv 2607.27191 — "shadow evaluation" on two unpublished NeurIPS 2026 submissions; agents did the engineering but "could not make substantial progress" on the research questions. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27191 **[search-verified]**
21. **Novikov et al. / DeepMind (2025), "AlphaEvolve"** (May 2025; results repo https://github.com/google-deepmind/alphaevolve_results ) — 4x4 complex matmul in 48 multiplications; 23% Pallas kernel speedup (AI improving AI training infrastructure). **[search-verified]**; **Gottweis et al. / Google (Feb 2025), "Towards an AI co-scientist"** https://research.google/blog/accelerating-scientific-breakthroughs-with-an-ai-co-scientist/ — wet-lab-validated AML drug repurposing and liver fibrosis targets. **[search-verified]**; **Sakana (2025), "The AI Scientist-v2"** arXiv 2504.08066 — first fully AI-generated ICLR workshop paper (score 6.33). **[search-verified]**; **FutureHouse "Robin"** (2025) https://www.futurehouse.org/research-announcements/demonstrating-end-to-end-scientific-discovery-with-robin-a-multi-agent-system **[search-verified]**
22. ★ **Kokotajlo, Alexander, Larsen, Lifland & Dean (April 2025), "AI 2027"** and its **Takeoff Forecast** https://ai-2027.com/research/takeoff-forecast — superhuman-coder milestone -> ASI median ~1 year; Kokotajlo has since pushed timelines toward ~2030. **[search-verified]**

24. **Kulveit et al. (2025), "Gradual Disempowerment,"** arXiv 2501.16946 — the structural (non-takeover) loss-of-control story. **[search-verified]**

## 4. Key things to know

**Core vocabulary**
1. **Recursive self-improvement (RSI):** AI improving its own training/architecture so gains compound; Anthropic's operational version is "an AI system capable of fully autonomously designing and developing its own successor."
2. **Software intelligence explosion (SIE) vs hardware bottleneck:** rapid progress from algorithms/data/scaffolding alone with fixed compute (Eth & Davidson) vs the view that experiments and chips bind (Whitfill & Wu; Favaro & Clark's "chips, grids, bandwidth").
3. **Takeoff speed:** fast/slow (Bostrom), continuous/discontinuous (Christiano); modern usage: time from "superhuman coder" to ASI (AI 2027: ~1 year median).
4. **Returns to software R&D (r):** in Davidson's model, if doubling cumulative research effort more than doubles capability (r>1) the loop accelerates; estimates hinge on Bloom et al.-style diminishing returns.
5. **AI R&D progress multiplier / uplift:** how much faster AI progress is with AI help (Greenblatt: ~1.4-1.6x serial engineering speedup, ~1.15-1.2x overall as of Apr 2026 https://blog.redwoodresearch.org/p/my-picture-of-the-present-in-ai **[fetched]**; Anthropic Aug 2026: "not yet by a factor of 2").
6. **AI R&D capability threshold:** RSP v3's "compress two years of 2018-2024 AI progress into a single year," operationalized as full substitution for research staff within 5x cost, or a doubling of aggregate capability progress attributable to automation.
7. **Task/time horizon:** length of human-expert task an agent completes at 50% success; doubled every ~7 months 2019-25, ~3.5 months in 2024-25.
8. **Baumol effect / weak links / bottlenecks:** growth set by the slowest essential task (Aghion-Jones-Jones; Jones-Tonetti); Cowen's "humans are the bottleneck."
9. **Ideas getting harder to find:** research productivity falls as fields mature; the drag term in every explosion model.
10. **Compute overhang:** if algorithms improve faster than compute is used, existing hardware can suddenly support far more capable systems.
11. **Superhuman coder milestone:** AI 2027's gateway to automation of AI research (Mar 2027 in the scenario).
12. **Country of geniuses in a datacenter:** Amodei's image of millions of Nobel-level instances.
13. **Tech tree:** which sciences accelerate first as a function of data availability, evaluation signals, tacit knowledge (TAI agenda wording).
14. **Jagged frontier:** capability varies sharply across superficially similar tasks (Dell'Acqua et al.); TAI extends this to misalignment of private and social returns across fields.
15. **Evals saturation / Goodharting:** Anthropic's Aug 2026 report says task-based R&D evals have "saturated," forcing reliance on internal telemetry and surveys; benchmark validity becomes a policy problem.
16. **Differential progress / intervention points:** deliberately steering which capabilities advance first, and where a lab or government could slow the loop.

**Key tensions / open questions**

1. **Software explosion vs compute/experiment bottleneck.** Pro-explosion: Eth & Davidson, Davidson & Houlden, AI 2027, Clark (60% by 2028). Skeptics: Whitfill & Wu's complements specification; Ord on generation time; Favaro & Clark on physical constraints; Greenblatt's ~1.2x overall multiplier; Princeton's shadow evaluation. *Say:* the disagreement is mostly about two parameters (r, and the compute–labor elasticity of substitution) and one process fact (how much of frontier progress requires serial large-scale experiments) — and propose measuring them.
2. **Are measured productivity effects real?** METR RCT (-19%, 2025) vs lab claims (80% of code; 8x LOC; 4x self-reported with Mythos Preview) vs METR's production-function reading (>2x plausible) vs METR's 2026 follow-up (~18% speedup but severe selection). *Say:* self-reports overestimate; LOC overestimates; RCTs under-sample high-uplift tasks; the honest answer is a range with the lab-internal aggregate-progress measure (Anthropic's "leading indicators") as the target statistic.
3. **How to measure aggregate AI research speed (telemetry).** Candidates: internal capability-per-effective-compute trends; Epoch's algorithmic-efficiency series; the 60-task O*NET-style taxonomy; Chan et al.'s capital/time-allocation metrics; CSET's indicator list; RSP's "double the rate of progress." *Say:* distinguish input measures (share of research labor automated) from output measures (capability per compute), and note lag.
4. **Which early-warning indicators are credible?** Anthropic itself says saturation has reduced its confidence; METR demanded better surveys and leading indicators. *Say:* prefer indicators that are hard to Goodhart, observable by third parties, and that move before the threshold (e.g., agent-authored PR share on frontier-relevant infra, experiment-queue throughput, time-horizon on RE-Bench-like tasks).
5. **Oversight and intervention rights; are fire drills useful?** Favaro & Clark call for a coordinated pause mechanism; TAI asks who decides; International AI Safety Report 2026 flags "minimal empirical understanding" of the feedback loop https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2026 **[search-verified]**. *Say:* tabletop exercises are valuable only if they exercise real decision rights (board, government) and are scored against pre-registered triggers.
6. **Explosive growth vs Baumol.** 30%+ growth (Erdil-Besiroglu, Davidson) vs Cowen/Acemoglu/Jones "weak links." *Say:* both can be right sequentially — explosive progress in digitizable R&D, then bottlenecks in physical diffusion — which is exactly why TAI pairs R&D with a "diffusion" pillar.
7. **Which sciences accelerate first?** Verifiable, data-rich, fast-feedback fields (math, code, some chem/bio with in-silico evals) vs tacit, slow-feedback fields (clinical medicine, materials synthesis). Evidence: AlphaEvolve (math/kernels), co-scientist (drug repurposing with wet-lab validation), Robin; counter-evidence: Princeton agents on open-ended ML research. *Say:* propose an index.
8. **Private vs social returns.** Drug repurposing and defense-relevant domains may be under- or over-supplied; Anthropic's Aug 2026 interviews found no dramatic acceleration yet in non-AI domains. *Say:* frame as an externality-mapping problem.
9. **Evidence quality (Toner-Rodgers).** A single fabricated NBER paper shaped a year of discourse. *Say:* pre-registration, data provenance, and replication should be part of any TAI measurement design.
10. **Benchmark validity.** RSP v3 dropped pre-specified evals for an "affirmative case"; saturation is now explicit. *Say:* treat benchmarks as one noisy input into a latent "progress rate" model.
11. **Are lab timelines running ahead of evidence?** OpenAI's "automated AI research intern by September 2026" and "automated researcher by March 2028" https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/28/sam-altman-says-openai-will-have-a-legitimate-ai-researcher-by-2028/ vs MIT Tech Review's 18 Aug 2026 piece https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/08/18/1142188/ai-recursive-self-improvement/ **[fetched]**. *Say:* the intern-level milestone is engineering; the contested step is research taste.
