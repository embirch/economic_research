# A second long-list, pointed at three themes

The first long-list (`programme/LONGLIST.md`, 39 entries, 16 September) was generated with no steer from the human, by design, and the short-list of nine came from it. This note asks for a second, themed long-list. It relaxes the no-steer rule in one respect only: the themes below are given. The questions are not. The three gates are unchanged and every candidate must pass all three: (1) it rests on a gap the corpus leaves open, evidenced in `programme/LEDGER.md` or by a documented absence; (2) the data to answer it is confirmed to exist in the public releases, at the grain and with the thresholds stated, before the entry is written; (3) it is one standalone question with a why-it-matters an economist and a general reader would both recognise.

## The three themes, as directions

**T1. Women and AI.** How women use AI, what for, how it differs, and what determines who gets the benefit. This theme has a data problem stated below; candidates must say exactly which public object carries sex or gender and at what grain, or be framed so that the published aggregate figures are the object of study.

**T2. Staying human while using AI.** Collaboration over delegation; learning as a use; the personal, relational and emotional uses of Claude and where they concentrate; the attitudes and feelings people report; whether people keep judgement, skill and agency while using the tool. The corpus has both usage data and self-report data here, and a candidate that puts the two together is preferred to one that uses either alone.

**T3. Who gets the most out of AI, and why.** Not who uses it most, but who gets value from it: success rates, productivity, task value, expertise, tenure, education, and the gaps by place and income. The programme's standing taste applies: the reports describe, this programme explains. A candidate that names the mechanism and the test beats one that maps the pattern.

Across all three the register is the programme's: find the invisible force behind a published pattern, propose the mechanism, test it against all the units rather than the anecdote, and say whether it holds.

## What the human wants from the list

Twelve to twenty candidates, in the exact format of `programme/LONGLIST.md`, each carrying its gap (ledger id or documented absence), its data line (release, file, grain, threshold), its one question, its why-it-matters for both readers, its closest published answer and why that falls short, and a first note on the rival explanations. Entries that overlap the existing long-list must say which entry and what is new. No scoring yet; the sweep comes after the human has read the list.

## Division of work

The human's assistant prepares the data-availability check for the three themes and a first draft of candidates from the ledger and the wiki. The steward confirms data existence for each candidate at column level and corrects the data lines. The referee sweeps the confirmed list against the three gates, names inherited framings, and flags any candidate whose why-it-matters would not survive a null. The lead is not asked to generate this list; if the referee finds the draft thin on a theme, the lead is asked for additions on that theme only.
