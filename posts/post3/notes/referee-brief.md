## Assumptions sweep

**(1) Value judgement — needs a design change (item 1), otherwise handled.** "Leading edge" and "or just its largest share" are correctly dropped (§1). "Narrow" survives in the title while §7(i) bans "narrowing" — resolve now (item 1). "Casualisation" is carried as a reading, not a finding, correctly; but §12 substitutes "professional"/"routine"/"worth doing" for Anthropic's "casual AI users" (item 11).

**(2) Construct mapping — newly flagged (items 4, 5, 6).** What S measures, in Anthropic's words: "we classify conversations into tasks according to O\*NET, a US taxonomy that maps specific tasks to occupations and occupation groups (e.g., a task involving software debugging would fall into the Computer and Mathematical occupation group)" (`economic-index-2025-09-report`, 2025-09-15, p.20); the published level is "tasks associated with Computer and Mathematical occupations accounting for 35% of conversations on Claude.ai" (R5 p.5) on "2019 O\*NET-SOC codes, while previous reports use the 2010 vintage" (fn2 p.11); Anthropic's own gloss is "such (mostly) coding-related tasks" (R4 p.7). So S is the share of records whose assigned task is held by at least one SOC-15 occupation under full duplication and renormalisation — which on the 2019 recode includes a Bioinformatics Technicians system-administration task that is not coding by inspection and carries about a third of the published +14%. What C measures: the same shape as "Share of conversations assigned to the 10 most prevalent O\*NET tasks, by platform and report version" (R5 Fig. 1.1, p.5) on SOC-15's own mass — §7(ii) correctly says levels are not comparable to 19%/24%. Direction the sign could run without the mechanism: C rises if SOC-15 loses tail nodes at the 15-record floor (node count falls), if casual entrants bring a few generic tasks, or if the heaviest coders (diverse tasks) leave; C falls if the largest tasks migrate, if a new node set clears the floor in a larger counted base, or — on the API — if one session becomes many prompt-response records. The API unit is a prompt-response pair, not a conversation (item 5). The primary allocation rule is unnamed (item 4).

**(3) Composition or selection — newly flagged (item 5), otherwise handled.** Users: Claude.ai = Free/Pro (Aug) then Free/Pro/Max (Nov, Feb), a self-selected consumer base with a Super Bowl first-time inflow in February; 1P API = developer traffic including Claude Code (R5 fn1 p.11), sampled in August from a customer pool covering about half of usage, with the record being a call. Mixes that produce the pattern without the mechanism: on Claude.ai, casual entrants (H1 via a few generic tasks, or H3 via pure dilution) and the exit of Claude Code-using developers to the API (H1 or H2 depending on which tasks they carried); on the API, Claude Code's growth alone changes the within-SOC-15 call mix (H4's shape) with no change in any developer's coding. Occupation is inferred from the task, not the user (§7(ii), ATLAS §Traps 36) — handled; the brief says so and the design cannot separate "who" from "what" — handled and stated. Seychelles netted — handled. The Max entry on the placebo leg — newly flagged.

**(4) Anthropic's own results that cut against or bound — newly flagged (items 6, 7), otherwise handled.** Handled: Claude.ai whole-universe concentration fell 24% → 19% (R5 p.5); novel tasks "barely changed" (p.7); the vintage footnote (fn2 p.11). Newly flagged: Anthropic already reads the fall as dilution — "a standard 'adoption curve' story" (p.3), "more casual AI users" (p.6) — and as migration — "a decrease in coding as it shifts to the API" (p.8); an H3 result confirms a stated reading rather than separating an unstated one, and the contribution should be phrased that way. Against H4: "Since August 2025, 1P API usage has become more concentrated … 33% of traffic, up from 28%" (p.19), the mechanism having been offered for a Nov→Feb flatness (p.6). Against novelty: the April 2025 software-development report's within-coding task-type and language shares. Bounding the "leading indicator" frame: "As tasks migrate to the API, they may become more exposed to automation" (p.9) is Anthropic's conjecture and is quoted as such.
