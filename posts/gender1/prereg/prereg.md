## Definitions, fixed

### 1 · Outcome cells

The published percentage `Value` in Eurostat table `isoc_ai_iaiu` (official TSV of 21 September 2026, `data/cache/eurostat/isoc_ai_iaiu.tsv`, sha256 `7f668f7b…`; the EIGE mirror of the same date agrees on every cell), for indicator `indic_is ∈ {I_IUAI, I_IUAIPR, I_IUAIWP, I_IUAIFE}`, unit `unit ∈ {PC_IND, PC_IND_IU3, PC_IND_IUAI}`, geography `geo`, and population group `ind_type`. A cell is **usable** if its value is non-missing and its flag is not `u`. A **pair** is the female and male cells for the same geography, group suffix, indicator and unit; a pair is usable only if both cells are.

### 2 · Groups

- Overall: `F_Y16_74` and `M_Y16_74`.
- Age bands: `F_/M_` + `Y16_24, Y25_34, Y35_44, Y45_54, Y55_64, Y65_74`. Overlapping bands and `Y75_89` are never used.
- Education: `F_/M_` + `I0_2, I3_4, I5_8`.
- No other sex-prefixed group exists; employment, occupation, urbanisation, citizenship and age-by-education carry no sex prefix and are not used.

### 3 · Quantities

For a usable pair with female rate `p_F` and male rate `p_M` (percentages):

- **gap** `= p_M − p_F` in percentage points; positive means higher male use.
- **ratio** `= p_F / p_M`; missing if `p_M = 0`.
- **standardised rate** for sex `s`: `Σ_b w_b p_{s,b}` over the six bands with the EU27 2025 sex-pooled population weights `w_b` from `demo_pjan` (script 01; provisional file, flag `ep`); defined only when all twelve band cells are usable; never renormalised over surviving bands; never imputed.
- **standardised gap** `= standardised male rate − standardised female rate`.
- **internet-composition share** by band `= gap on PC_IND − gap on PC_IND_IU3`.
- **purpose participation**: the purpose indicator on `PC_IND`; **purpose among users**: the same on `PC_IND_IUAI`.

### 4 · Sample rule, sets and exclusions

- **Primary set:** the EU27 member states (27 geographies). `EU27_2020` and `EA` are reference aggregates, never units in a count or a tercile.
- **Extension set:** AL, BA, CH, MK, NO, RS, TR, XK, reported separately and classified against the primary set's cut points, never inside them.
- **Usable-pair rule:** every comparison uses only geographies where every cell it needs is usable. Each figure and table states its geography count. Nothing under thirty geographies is reported as a percentage of geographies; counts only.
- **Flagged cells** enter one appendix table, marked, and no main result.
- **Common-geography rule:** any comparison of two measures (points against ratio, crude against standardised, one purpose against another, one denominator against another) is made on the intersection of usable geographies, stated.

### 5 · Error type and the bound

Eurostat publishes no cell-level standard errors, counts or design variables. No confidence interval is computed from published cells and no bootstrap of cells is presented as sampling uncertainty. The only uncertainty statement is the **sampling bound** of script 01: with the national achieved sample `n` (from the national reference metadata, achieved counts for CZ and IT, implied by the reference indicator's yes-count and proportion elsewhere; none for NL, MK, XK), half per sex (A1), simple random sampling (A2, a lower bound), the country's published overall rate (A4), and EU27 band shares for the sex-by-age cells (A3). The reference indicator's published standard errors are within 11% of the SRS value at the median across 30 countries, with Romania the outlier at 2.8 times, so A2 is close for national totals and is labelled a bound throughout.

Realised bound (script 01): the 95% half-width of a country's overall gap has median 2.75 points across 32 countries (IT 0.88, DE 1.66, MT 4.67); within an age band the median half-width is 6.3 to 7.6 points. Two countries' overall gaps are distinguishable at this bound only if they differ by more than roughly the sum of their half-widths.

### 6 · Frames, fixed

- Reference period: use in the three months before the interview; fieldwork by convention in the first quarter of 2025. No earlier wave exists; no trend statement is possible.
- "Gender gap" is the framing; the categories are the source's female and male.
- The EU27 aggregate is population-weighted and is reported as a reference, not compared with member states as a peer.

## Hypotheses and decision rules

Every rule is a count over geographies in the stated set and can fail. Each hypothesis is judged twice: on the **raw majority** of geographies, and on the **distinguishable majority**, which counts only geographies where the difference the rule compares exceeds that geography's bound for the quantity (the per-country or per-band half-width from `power_rules.json`); both counts are reported with their denominators.

### H-work · the gap is larger in work use than in private use

- **Test:** for each EU27 geography with usable pairs for `I_IUAIWP` and `I_IUAIPR` on `PC_IND`, compare `gap_work` with `gap_private`.
- **Rule for support:** `gap_work > gap_private` in a majority of the usable EU27 geographies. **Against:** a majority the other way, or a tie.
- **Disclosed prior evidence:** the EU27 aggregate has private 5.5 and work 3.0 points, against H-work; Henseke's work gap among workers (4.1 points) is on a different population and denominator. The test is kept because the country majority is unseen, and its verdict is reported with the disclosure that the aggregate pointed the other way.
- **Secondary form:** the same comparison among AI users (`PC_IND_IUAI`), reported beside it, not in the rule.
- **Expected resolution:** the difference `gap_work − gap_private` per country is distinguishable only beyond about the overall half-width (median 2.75 points); the distinguishable count will be smaller than the raw count, and both are reported.

### H-age · the gap is smallest or reversed at 16–24 and largest between 25 and 44

- **Test:** for each EU27 geography with all twelve usable sex-by-age cells for `I_IUAI` on `PC_IND` (at most 24 after IE is excluded; the 33-of-36 count includes the extension), compute the six band gaps.
- **Rule (a) for support:** the 16–24 gap is the smallest of the six, or negative, in a majority of usable geographies. **Rule (b) for support:** the largest of the six gaps lies in `Y25_34` or `Y35_44` in a majority. Each rule can fail on its own; H-age is **declared** only if both hold; **partly declared** if one holds; **not declared** otherwise.
- **Expected resolution:** band half-widths are 6 to 8 points, so the ordering of six gaps inside a country is mostly inside noise; the distinguishable count for (a) and (b) is expected to be small, and the post says so whichever way the raw count goes.

### H-education · the gap widens with education

- **Test:** for each EU27 geography with usable pairs for all three education groups on `I_IUAI` `PC_IND`, order the three gaps.
- **Rule for support:** the `I5_8` gap is the largest of the three in a majority. **Against:** otherwise.
- **Specification risk:** education cannot be age-standardised (no sex-by-age-by-education cells), and women's tertiary lead among the young means the high-education group is younger for women than for men; this is named in the interpretation, not adjusted.

### H-composition · applying a common age structure changes the class of few countries

- **Test:** on the geographies with a standardised gap (all twelve cells usable), classify each by the class rule below on the crude gap and on the standardised gap.
- **Rule for support:** at most one third of those geographies change class. **Against:** more than one third change.
- **Companion quantity, descriptive:** the change in the gap in points per country, reported never as a percentage explained.

### The class rule (fixed here, used by H-composition and by every "largest" statement)

On the EU27 primary set, on usable overall pairs, cut points are the terciles of the signed gap on `PC_IND`, of the ratio, and of the standardised gap (each on its own usable set). A geography is **large-gap** only if it is in the top tercile on all three; **small-gap** only if in the bottom tercile on all three; **reversed** only if `p_F > p_M` on `PC_IND` and on `PC_IND_IU3`; otherwise **not distinguishable**. Ties at a cut point within presentation rounding (0.1 point) go to "not distinguishable". The number of geographies that change class between any two of the three measures is reported. Extension geographies are classified against the EU27 cut points.

### The confirmatory set, counted

Five confirmatory rules: H-work (1), H-age (2), H-education (1), H-composition (1). No p-value is computed anywhere, so there is no multiple-testing correction; the exposure is five count rules on one dataset, stated. Descriptive, not confirmatory: the map (rates and gaps by geography), the class table, the internet-composition share by band, purpose among users, the education profile. Exploratory, labelled: the two triangulation legs.

### Two implementations

Every gap, ratio, standardised rate and class is computed twice: once with pandas on the long table, once in pure Python from the TSV by key; they must agree to 1e-9 (script 04). The check block fails otherwise.

### Synthetic recovery (script 04)

A synthetic table with known female and male rates per band and known EU weights is built; the standardised gap must recover the implanted value to 1e-9; a table with equal male and female rates must return zero gaps, ratio one, and every geography "not distinguishable"; a table with an implanted composition effect (identical band gaps, different age structures) must return a crude gap that differs from the standardised one by the implanted amount.

## Robustness, fixed now

1. Flagged cells included, in an appendix table only.
2. Ratio against signed gap: class changes counted.
3. Internet-user denominator (`PC_IND_IU3`) for every overall and band comparison; the internet-composition share by band.
4. AI-user denominator (`PC_IND_IUAI`) for purposes.
5. EU27 against the extension set, and against the full 35.
6. Equal band weights as a sensitivity to the population weights, labelled as a sensitivity, never as the standardisation.
7. Triangulation, exploratory: rank correlation (Spearman, with the geography count) and class agreement between the EU27 ordering by Eurostat overall gap and (i) OpenAI Signals' feminine share of messages by country, June 2025 (26 of 27), (ii) Henseke's work-adoption gap by country, against the Eurostat work-use gap. Reported whichever way.
8. Purpose-specific standardisation only if all twelve band cells are usable for that purpose in at least fifteen EU27 geographies; otherwise omitted and said so.

Nothing is added to this list without a dated deviation.

## Interpretation, fixed now

- **All four hypotheses declared and few class changes:** the European gender gap in AI use is concentrated in work and mid-life, smallest or reversed among the young, widens with education, and survives age structure; the map's classes are stable; the close names the returns question and the non-use post.
- **Hypotheses mixed or failed:** the gap is real at the EU level and its location below it depends on the purpose and the band asked about; the post reports where the literature's expectation failed and says that no single number should be carried into an opportunity model.
- **Most comparisons not distinguishable:** the finding is the bound: a country ranking of the gender gap cannot be supported from published cells at this precision, and the post states what Eurostat would need to publish (cell counts or standard errors) for it to be.
- **H-composition fails (many class changes):** the published country ordering is partly an age-structure ordering, which is the measurement finding this post was designed to catch.

In every outcome: the EU27 aggregate values disclosed above are reported as the reference; the reversed geographies are named as a class, not ranked; no adjacent-rank statement appears; the close carries no number the body has not.
