Data description The EU survey on the use of ICT in households and by individuals is an annual survey conducted since 2002. In Bulgaria, it has been conducted since 2004. In 2025, the survey collects data on the access to information and communication technologies (ICT), on the use of the internet, e-government, electronic (eID) and e-commerce as well as e-skills and privacy and protection of personal data. 3.1.1. Survey name in national and English languages National language : Изследване за използването на информационни и комуникационни технологии (ИКТ) в домакинствата и от лицата през 2025 година English : EU survey on the use of ICT in households and by individuals Questionnaire(s) in national language(s) and the translation in English are available in the Annexes below. 3.2. Classification system The following common concepts and definitions apply under the Integrated European Social Statistics (IESS): the International Standard Classification of Education (ISCED) 2011 published in the following breakdowns: low (ISCED levels 0-2: no formal education, primary education or lower secondary education), medium (ISCED levels 3-4: upper secondary or post-secondary non-tertiary education) and high (ISCED levels 5-6: tertiary programmes which normally need a successful completion of ISCED 3 or 4, or second-stage tertiary education leading to an advanced research qualification); the International Standard Classification for Occupation ISCO-08 at the 2-digit level; the Classification of Economic Activities (NACE Rev.2-2008), at section level; the Common classification of territorial units for statistics ( NUTS 1 ) – finer granularity of NUTS 2 is provided on optional basis by some Member States; the SCL – Geographical code list; information about household income is provided at lower level of detail. Additional classifications used in the national questionnaire : No additional classifications are used in the national questionnaire. 3.3. Coverage - sector The ICT survey in households and by individuals covers those households having at least one member in the age group 16 to 74 years old. Individuals aged 75 to 89 are covered by some countries on voluntary basis. Internet access of households refers to the percentage of households that have an internet access, so that anyone in the household could use the internet. 3.3.1. Differences in scope at national level No deviations between national survey and the scope of Eurostat. 3.4. Statistical concepts and definitions The survey is collecting data of internet users, individuals who have used the internet in the three months prior to the survey. This annual survey is used to benchmark ICT-driven developments, both by following developments for core variables over time and by looking in greater depth at other aspects at a specific point in time. While the survey initially concentrated on access and connectivity issues, its scope has subsequently been extended to cover a variety of subjects (for example, the use of internet, e-government, use of eID, e-commerce, e-skills and privacy and protection of personal data) and socio-economic analysis (such as regional diversity, gender specificity, differences in age, education and the employment situation). The scope of the survey with respect to different technologies is also adapted to cover new product groups and means of delivering communication technologies to end-users. For more details on the methodology applicable in each survey year, please consult the Compiler's Manual for the respective year. Source data The source of the raw data is described with more details in the paragraphs below. 18.1.1. Sampling frame The sampling frame for the ICT survey is a subset of the General Population frame used for sampling for all social surveys carried out by the NSI. The frame is based on the 2021 Population Census database and is updated annually with the current demographic statistics as of 31 December (deaths and new-borns). The sampling frame consists of all individuals aged between 16 and 74 living in private households. Individuals living in collective establishments are excluded. However, in 2025 the sampling frame is enlarged with population aged between 75 and 89. This is done intentionally to observe and collect information about this elderly part of the population. The sample is not drawn from any other survey sample, master sample or micro-census. The survey on the use of ICT in households and by individuals is a stand-alone survey and it is carried out on a voluntary basis. There are shortcomings of the sampling frame in terms of uninhabited dwellings, new buildings not included in the sampling frame, internal migration and other frame errors. 18.1.2. Sampling design Sampling units for the survey on the use of ICT are households selected by a two-stage stratified clustered random sampling. The sample is stratified by NUTS 3 area (28 districts) and by location (urban/rural). At the first stage, the primary sampling units (Census Enumeration Areas) are selected by systematic selection with a probability proportional to the size. At the second stage, 6 households per PSU are selected by simple random sampling. All members of the selected households aged between 16 and 89 years are surveyed. The survey does not have a longitudinal component. 18.1.3. Net effective sample size Restricted from publication 18.2. Frequency of data collection Annual 18.3. Use of imputation methods Methods used to impute item non-response: No imputation methods are used. 18.5.3. Grossing-up procedures Grossing up procedures have been applied to Individuals and/or Households: Individuals and Households Description of the weighting procedures : Weighting for households and individuals in the age group 16 to 74 years old The sample is drawn from a frame containing all individuals aged 16 to 89 years living in private households, but for the purposes of weighting the data for households and individuals in the age group 16 to 74 years old, all individuals aged 75 - 89 are removed from the net sample. The first step of the weighting procedure is the calculation of design weights, which represent the inverse of inclusion probabilities of the sample units. According to the country specifics, the sample is stratified to 56 strata by NUTS 3 (28 regions) and location (urban/rural). The next step is adjustment of the non-response. The procedure used is “weighting classes”. The classes are defined by the sampling design strata. The reason for this is the limited information available for the non-responding households. The final step is calibration of the non-response weights to population totals. Calibration is done using the SAS Macro Calmar 2. The main source for calibration totals is the Information System “Demography”. Another source of information is the 2021 Population Census database. As a result, individuals in the net sample are weighted to represent the population as of 31 December 2024 by the groups defined below. The calibration is applied on both household and individual level: Household level: Number of households by NUTS 3 region (28 groups) Individual level: 6 age groups (16-24, 25-34, 35-44, 45-54, 55-64, 65-74) by sex (12 groups) Number of persons by NUTS 3 region and location (56 groups). An integrative calibration design is applied. As a result, all individuals of a specific household receive equal weights and these weights correspond to the calculated household weights. The calibration method used is “Logit” method (m=3 in Calmar) where the lower bound for g-weights is 0.1 and upper bound is 5. Weighting for households and individuals in the age group 75 to 89 years old After the above calibration procedure, the surveyed individuals aged 75 to 89 years old are returned to the net sample and assigned the calculated household weight. This procedure allows to estimate the number of individuals aged 75 - 89 living in households with individuals aged 16 - 74. Having this information in mind, an additional calibration step is performed for individuals living in households composed only by people aged 75 - 89. The calibration is applied on both household and individual level: Household level: Number of households in the country by urban and rural (2 groups) Individual level: 1 age group 75-89 by sex (2 groups). An integrative calibration design is applied. As a result, all individuals of a specific household receive equal weights and these weights correspond to the calculated household weights. The calibration method used is “Logit” method (m=3 in Calmar) where the lower bound for g-weights is 0.2 and upper bound is 1.5. Combining these two weighting procedures allows us to obtain an unbiased estimation for both individuals aged 16 to 74 and those aged 75 to 89. 18.6. Adjustment Not relevant 18.6.1.
