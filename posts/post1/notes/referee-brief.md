**1. The decision rules do not partition the outcome space, and H1 has no smallest effect of interest. — BLOCKING. `BRIEF.md` §6 (all four signatures and counter-results), §9(1), §9(3), §9(4), §12.**
Checked: can each rule fail; do the four rules together give every outcome one owner; is the size criterion the same across rivals. Found four criteria doing one job. (a) H1/H2: "positive [negative] and outside the MDE in all three waves", the MDE being 0.42–0.43 pp under the conversation-level model (I reproduce it: SE 0.149–0.151 pp at p ≈ 0.46–0.51 on 199k–249k classified conversations per quartile). (b) H4: 95% interval inside ±1 pp in all three waves, with "any wave whose interval excludes zero with a consistent sign" counting against it. (c) H1's counter-result "a difference inside the permutation null's central 95%" — a task-level test whose band is of the order of the steward's design-based MDE (12.5–17.4 pp), because the top quartile holds 11.5–19 effective tasks. (d) H3: the gradient "falls inside [the MDE] when SOC-15 tasks are excluded", against H1's own counter-result "loses more than half its size or its sign" for the same event. Consequences: a Q4−Q1 gap of +0.6 pp with interval [0.3, 0.9] in every wave satisfies H1's signature and H4's margin at once, and H4's zero clause then hands it to H1 — so the post would close with "the work people hand over to Claude outright is the expensive work" (§12) on a gap that moves the wage-weighted automation share by about 0.07 pp (item 4). The ±1 pp margin is therefore not the operative rule for H4 either: with a 0.3 pp half-width, H4 is in practice |Δ| ≲ 0.3 pp, and the zone between 0.3 pp and 1 pp belongs to nobody. A gradient of +0.8 pp in two waves and −0.2 pp in the third goes "to H3 or H4" (§9(1)) and fits neither signature. If (c) binds, H1 is declarable only above a 10–17 pp gap, a different design from §9(4)'s; if it does not bind, it should not sit in a counter-result. H3 as written is unreachable: a gradient carried 90% by SOC-15 still sits far outside 0.42 pp after exclusion.
Must change: (i) one size criterion for all four, keyed to a stated smallest effect of interest rather than to the MDE — the natural candidate is the ±1 pp margin already in §9(4): H1 if the interval's lower bound exceeds +1 pp in all three waves, H2 its mirror, H4 if the interval lies inside ±1 pp in all three; (ii) two further outcomes named and given §12 endings — "interval excludes zero but crosses the margin" (a gradient too small to change the wage-bill reading) and "sign not persistent across waves" (no persistent gradient); (iii) H3 keyed to the fraction of the gradient lost on exclusion and within group (H1's "more than half"), not to "inside the MDE"; (iv) the permutation null given one role — either a confirmatory gate with its own MDE stated in §9 (then §12 must say what the design resolves as a statement about tasks), or a task-level bound reported in §10 and removed from H1's counter-result; (v) the margin stated in the quantity §2 says matters (item 4) so the lead chooses it knowing what 1 pp buys. The editor's (b) point — a branch with a test and no ending — is this item in another form: every named hypothesis has an ending, but the outcomes the rules will actually produce do not.

## Assumptions sweep

**(1) Value judgement in the framing — "cheap", "expensive", "delegated". Newly flagged; needs a decision now (item 3).** "Delegate" is Anthropic's word for directive use (2025-09, p. 3). "Cheap work" and "expensive work" are not in the corpus, which writes "task value", "less remunerated tasks", "high-value, complex work", "tasks that command lower wages". §7(1)'s handling rests on the editor's mistaken premise that they are. The frame prices work by the wage of the people who do it, which the brief states correctly, and "cheap" is relative to Claude's task mix: the bottom quartile's ceiling ($24–26/hr) sits above the median US occupation wage on the same file (≈ $22.9/hr, group-employment-weighted). The body rule ("the wage of the work"; no direction graded) is right; the title needs the decision.

**(2) Construct mapping against Anthropic's verbatim definition. Newly flagged (items 1, 4, 5).** The measure: "Automation encompasses interaction patterns focused on task completion: Directive: Users give Claude a task and it completes it with minimal back-and-forth; Feedback Loops: Users automate tasks and provide feedback to Claude as needed" (`economic-index-2025-09-report`, 2025-09, PDF p. 9); "automation_pct: Percentage of classifiable collaboration that is automation-focused (directive, feedback loop patterns)" (`release_2025_09_15/data_documentation.md`); the classifier's instruction, "Directive – Human delegates complete task execution to AI with minimal interaction" (`economic-index-2025-02-paper`, Appendix F.3, p. 25); Anthropic's caveats, "users might edit and adjust the response they receive from Claude outside the chat window" and "even automation of simple tasks can serve to enhance human capabilities when embedded within broader human-directed workflows" (ibid., p. 9); and the distinction from autonomy, "'Translate this paragraph into French' is high automation (directive, minimal back-and-forth) but low AI autonomy" (2026-01, p. 19). It measures the shape of one dialogue, not whether labour was displaced. The brief carries the autonomy distinction and the "not a count of tasks automated" point; it does not carry the out-of-chat and embedded-workflow caveats, which are what make §2's wage-bill reading a model statement rather than a measurement. Which way the sign can run by construction: toward H2, because high-wage conversations have 1.53× the turns (2026-06, p. 13) and directive is "minimal back-and-forth", and because one-shot personal requests are directive; toward H1, because "debug errors" is automative by definition (2025-03) and coding is the top quartile — which is H3, not H1. The wage construct — "the average hourly wage of US workers who perform that task" (2026-03, p. 8), "weighting by employment and the fraction of time spent on that task" (footnote 5, p. 11) — is a rate, not a bill; the rank-based design and the stated non-reproduction are correctly handled, subject to item 10.
