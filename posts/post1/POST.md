# Is AI delegated more on low-wage work or on high-wage work?

*September 2026 · Evidence from Claude*

## The puzzle Anthropic left open

Two chapters of the Anthropic Economic Index answer to different halves of this question and have never been crossed. One publishes the collaboration facet — whether a conversation on a given task was worked as automation or as augmentation — at task grain, wave after wave. The other prices the task mix, "the average hourly wage of US workers who perform that task" (Economic Index report, March 2026, p. 8), and then sorts model choice and compute by that price. The wage of the work has been crossed with how much work there is, and with which model does it. It has been crossed with how much back-and-forth the work took — in conversations mapped to higher-wage occupations users take more turns, a pattern the June 2026 report reads, conditionally, as looking "more labor-augmenting than labor-displacing" — but not with the collaboration facet itself, the measure that defines delegation.

The gap matters to one reader in particular: anyone who takes a published automation share into a model. The published share counts conversations and weights every task alike. A framework that cares about labour payments weights tasks by what they pay. So if delegation sits at one end of the wage distribution, reading the unweighted share as though it were weighted carries an error whose sign nobody has established. This post measures that error in three windows and finds it small and of no fixed sign — and then finds that its size and its sign are not the interesting part.

Three things a reader should hold before the first table. **First, the direction.** In each of three Claude.ai windows between August 2025 and February 2026, conversations on the top quarter of tasks by hourly wage were worked as directive or feedback-loop conversations at a higher rate than conversations on the bottom quarter. The difference is positive in every window; it is not the same size in any two of them. **Second, what that is worth as a model input.** Re-weighting a published automation share by the hourly wage of the work moves it by less than a percentage point in every window, and not in a consistent direction — it is a small correction of unfixed sign, and it is a correction to an hourly rate, not to a bill, because no hours enter it. **Third, the part that decides how the first two should be read.** The excess is carried by Computer & Mathematical tasks. Set that family aside and the top quarter is delegated *less* than the bottom; hold the occupational group fixed and the difference is negative in every window. For a modeller reading these windows, the quantity to condition on is the coding share of the task mix rather than its pay.

That is a two-sided answer, and the question is posed as a question for that reason. It is also a smaller question than it sounds. The unit here is a conversation on Claude.ai, and the occupation behind a task is inferred from the task, not from the user, so a task's wage is the wage of work that *resembles* the request rather than the wage of whoever made it. "Low-wage" and "high-wage" mean the bottom and top usage-weighted wage quartile of the tasks brought to Claude, not of the economy: Anthropic bounds its own data the same way — "Claude is used for high-value, complex work that is not broadly representative of the US economy" (March 2026, p. 19). None of what follows is a statement about work in general, and the close says how far short of one it falls.

## How to tell the explanations apart

The measured quantity is **D**, the usage-weighted automation share of the top wage quartile minus that of the bottom, in percentage points, estimated separately in each of the three windows and never pooled. A smallest effect of interest of one percentage point was fixed in advance, and an ordered rule — first match winning — assigns D's outcome to exactly one owner, so that no result is unowned and none has two owners. A rival *explanation* of a difference, rather than a rival value of D, is evaluated only once a difference is declared.

| Explanation | What it says | The signature only it predicts | What the three windows show |
|---|---|---|---|
| **H1 · Price** | delegation rises with the wage of the work | D's lower bound above +1 pp in all three windows, **and** D keeping its sign and more than half its size on every composition leg testable in that window | The lower bound clears +1 pp in two windows and not in the third; the leg clause fails in every window |
| **H2 · Inverse** | the high-wage work is the collaborative work | D's upper bound below −1 pp in all three windows | Not found: D is positive in all three |
| **O-A · A persistent difference not shown to clear the margin** | the direction holds in every window; the size is not shown to clear a point in every window | after H1 and H2 fail, all three intervals excluding zero with the same sign | **Declared** |
| **H4 · No relation at the margin** | the share does not track the wage by as much as a point | all three intervals inside ±1 pp *and* not all excluding zero with one sign | Not found |
| **O-B · No persistent difference** | — | anything else | Not found |
| **H3 · Composition, not price** | a declared difference is carried by a mix that travels with wage — the coding family, or the use-case mix | a declared D losing its sign, or more than half its size, on at least one composition leg, in every window in which that leg is testable | **Declared**, on the Computer & Mathematical leg and the within-group leg, each firing in all three windows |

The three composition legs are (a) the same difference with Computer & Mathematical tasks excluded and the quartile boundaries kept, (b) the difference averaged within SOC major group, and (e) the difference on work-dominant tasks — the use-case rival, testable in November and February only, because no facet name in the August file contains `use_case` — the pre-registration's (c), the leave-one-group-out series, and (d), the use-case mix by quartile, are descriptive and are reported beside the legs, not as legs.

## In each of three Claude.ai windows the top wage quartile was delegated more than the bottom, and only the sign is steady

"Delegated" here is Anthropic's automation share and nothing wider: a task's `directive` plus `feedback loop` conversations divided by its five classified collaboration patterns (`directive`, `feedback loop`, `learning`, `task iteration`, `validation`) in the `onet_task::collaboration` intersection at global grain. The `none` and `not_classified` patterns sit outside that denominator, so each quartile's `none` share is printed beside it below. Anthropic's own bounds on the construct travel with it: automation is not autonomy, and one dialogue's shape does not show what happened outside the chat window. The shares themselves are not monotone in the wage: in every window the share falls from the bottom quartile to the second and rises to the top — 53.1, 47.0, 49.8 and 54.4% in August (`none` 3.6, 1.9, 1.9 and 1.2); 46.6, 41.3, 46.0 and 54.0% in November (`none` 3.1, 1.1, 1.9 and 1.2); 48.8, 41.6, 43.5 and 49.5% in February (`none` 3.4, 1.1, 2.2 and 1.1).

Against those levels, the top-minus-bottom contrast is the finding. **In each of the three Claude.ai windows the automation share of conversations on top-wage-quartile tasks exceeded that of conversations on bottom-quartile tasks: by 1.4 points (4–11 August 2025), 7.4 points (13–20 November 2025) and 0.7 points (5–12 February 2026).** The intervals are two-sided 95% under a conversation-level binomial with the task mix held fixed; no release carries a user, session or account identifier, so they are a lower bound on the sampling variance. On the corrected quartile rule, which shares the boundary wage between the two adjacent quartiles instead of letting sort order decide, the differences are 0.5, 7.2 and 0.3 points, and August no longer exceeds one point. The size of the difference is not stable across windows: it varies from two thirds of a point to more than seven points, a spread twenty-six times the within-window standard error, so the intervals describe conversation sampling inside a window and not the variation between windows. Each window is estimated separately and never pooled, so a change in who is using Claude between windows sits inside that persistence test rather than being controlled for. And a task's rate is a usage-weighted blend over countries that cannot be cleaned at this grain; the geography that can be bounded, Seychelles in November, moves the difference by up to three points and does not account for November's excess.

Counting every Seychelles conversation on a top-quartile task as automation and every one on a bottom-quartile task as augmentation, with the weights netted, leaves November's difference at +4.5 points. Two further features of the top quartile belong beside the number rather than after it. The third quartile boundary is $43.40 an hour in every window — the wage the file assigns to Computer Occupations, All Other — and the 99 / 106 / 111 tasks at that wage carry 8 to 11 percentage points of a window, so the top quartile's membership is decided partly inside the coding family. And the top quartile is small where it counts: the Kish effective number of tasks is 94 / 83 / 126 on the analysis set and 10.6 / 8.9 / 16.4 in the top quartile.

## The excess is Computer & Mathematical work: with that family excluded, the top wage quartile on Claude.ai is delegated less than the bottom

The composition rule was registered because the coding family is most of the best-paid quarter of the work in this data and is the work Anthropic's own gloss of automative use names — "asking the model to directly complete a task or debug errors" (Economic Index report, March 2025). It is also most of what the top quartile is. **The excess is carried by Computer & Mathematical tasks, which hold 73, 71 and 65% of the top quartile's Claude.ai usage in the three windows and 43, 40 and 36% of the analysis set's.**

**With Computer & Mathematical tasks excluded and the quartile boundaries kept, the top quartile's automation share was 13.0, 10.5 and 11.9 points *below* the bottom quartile's.** That comparison is of task types, not of pay: what is left of the top quartile is managerial, scientific, engineering and clinical work carrying 6.1, 6.5 and 7.8 percentage points of usage mass, set against clerical, educational and sales work in the bottom quartile. The leave-one-group-out series is the same evidence seen from the other side, not a second test — **removing any single major group other than Computer & Mathematical moves the difference by at most 3.2, 2.3 and 2.9 points; removing Computer & Mathematical moves it by 14.4, 17.9 and 12.6.**

The second leg holds the occupational group fixed instead of deleting one. **Within SOC major group — averaging the top-minus-bottom difference over the ten (August, November) or nine (February) groups that hold tasks in both extreme quartiles, weighted by their usage in those quartiles — the difference was −4.2, −1.9 and −6.2 points; those groups carry 80, 80 and 76% of the two quartiles' mass and the other twelve or thirteen groups are not identified.** A third, blunter cut agrees: **removing the ten largest tasks by usage — one fifth to one quarter of named mass — turns the difference to −11.6, −13.1 and −11.4 points.**

**The pre-registered composition rule therefore fires: on both the SOC-15-excluded and the within-group leg the difference loses its sign in every window, and the post reads the relation between delegation on Claude.ai and the wage of the work as a mix that travels with wage — coding — rather than as a property of the wage.** The price mechanism is not refuted by this; it is unidentified, because the wage attached to a task is the wage of the occupation the task resembles, and the design cannot separate the two at any grain.

The use-case rival — that the bottom quartile's conversations are largely personal and coursework requests, and a one-shot personal request is directive by construction — is the one composition story the data do not settle. **Restricting to work-dominant tasks (work at least half of a task's published use-case cells; 943 and 1,071 tasks) leaves the difference at 3.7 points in November and 2.8 in February, keeping the sign and at least half the size, so the use-case leg does not fire — in November the retained fraction is 0.50 with a contrast interval straddling zero, so whether it keeps more or less than half is undetermined; August publishes no use-case facet and is untested.** A leg that does not fire is nothing shown, not a rival excluded, and this leg is not independent of the first: the work-dominant top quartile is the coding top quartile, 81 to 83% of its mass kept against 20 to 30% of the bottom quartile's. The mix it is drawn from is stark on its own terms — **the work share of the bottom quartile's conversations is 33% (November) and 29% (February) against 61% in the top; the bottom quartile is 45–56% personal requests.**

One exploratory reading is recorded because it bears on the word in the question, and it is exploratory, in no decision rule: **the `directive` pattern alone is lower in the top quartile in every window (−7.0, −9.5, −9.9 points) while `feedback loop` is higher (+8.4, +16.9, +10.6); the positive difference in the automation share is a feedback-loop difference.** Both the reversal outside coding and the lower `directive` share at the top are consistent with that turns reading — consistent with, not confirmation of it — and the U-shape of Figure 2 echoes the one published cross of the collaboration facet with occupational category, which was not monotone in wage either.

## Wage-weighting the published automation share of Claude.ai conversations moves it by less than a point, and not in one direction

This is the quantity the puzzle asked for. **Weighting the published automation share by the hourly wage of the work would move it by −0.02 points in August (an interval containing zero), +0.84 in November and −0.15 in February — below one point in every window, and in the same direction as the quartile gap in one window of three.** It is an hourly rate, not a bill: no hours enter it, and the wage is the wage of the occupation a task resembles. The one-percentage-point line it is measured against is the brief's own materiality line for this quantity, set separately from the one-point margin the quartile difference is judged on. August's null is a tight one: the minimum detectable difference there is 0.05 points, so a wage-weighting error as large as a twentieth of a point would have been seen in that window and was not. In levels: **the published five-pattern automation shares of the analysis set are 51.1, 47.0 and 45.9%; wage-weighted they are 51.1, 47.8 and 45.7%.**

So the correction is small in every window, indistinguishable from zero in one, and cannot be applied in a known direction from three windows: its sign follows the quartile difference's in one window of three, because Δ_W depends on all four quartiles and the trough of the U sits in the middle.

## What this means

In these three windows, the wage of the work is not what orders delegation on Claude.ai; the kind of work is. And the kind of work that sits at the top of the wage distribution in this data is software. A reader who has been treating a published automation share as a wage-blind average, and wondering how much that costs, now has the answer in both parts: the wage-weighted version of the number is close enough to the unweighted one, in these windows, that the choice between them is not what a conclusion turns on, and the reason it is close is not that pay is irrelevant but that the one composition that moves the number is a family of occupations, not a point on a pay scale.

The direction is not corroborated on the second wage source in any window, because BLS-EP does not price the computer occupations; its rebuild is the coding exclusion again. In each of the three Claude.ai windows the top wage quartile was delegated more than the bottom, without the interval clearing a point in every window, and on the corrected quartile rule the excess is smaller still; only the sign persists, and not the size. Weighting a published automation share by the wage of the work moves it by less than a point in every window and not in a single direction — an hourly rate, not a bill — so the correction is small, the direction is the finding, and the direction is coding.

What that leaves is a statement about a mix rather than about a price. The relation between delegation on Claude.ai and the wage of the work reads as a mix that travels with wage: coding, which is most of the best-paid quarter of the work in this data and the work Anthropic's own gloss of automative use names. The use-case leg does not fire and does not settle it. What looks like a statement about the wage distribution is a statement about software, and anyone conditioning an exposure or automation parameter on pay should be conditioning it on the coding share of the task mix instead.

A claim about work in general is not licensed by any of this. Read as a statement about tasks rather than about these windows' conversations, a design that resamples tasks resolves nothing below about fourteen to twenty percentage points. What would move the reading is specific and nameable: a later window in which the difference survives the modal-holder wage rule, the drop of the ten largest tasks and the fractional quartile rule at once, with a size inside the range these three windows have already shown; or a wage source that prices the computer family, so that the second-source test is a real one; or a window with a published use-case facet in which the work-dominant restriction can be run where it has never been run.

Until then the honest form of the answer is the question's own words, both halves of them. On Claude.ai, high-wage work is delegated somewhat more often than low-wage work, and the whole of that excess is coding; take the coding family out and high-wage work is delegated less. Whichever half of that a reader carries away, what these windows measure is the kind of work; the wage is only how it was sorted.

## Recommendations to Anthropic

**The Economic Index team should publish the collaboration facet crossed with the wage quartile, and with the occupational group, in the release itself.** Both halves already ship in the same file for the same window; the cross is one join away and exists in no publication, which is why the sign of this correction was unknown until now. It would not settle the question where it is still open: the intersections are published at global grain only, so a cross at that grain still cannot separate a task's country mix from its content, and the one geography that can be bounded here moves the November difference by up to three points.

**Whoever maintains the observed-exposure measure should report its automation weight twice, once with Computer & Mathematical tasks included and once with them excluded.** That measure weights a task at one half plus one half of its automation share (labour-market-impacts appendix, March 2026, p. 3), which is wage-blind by construction; the results here say the automation share's top-minus-bottom wage difference is carried by one occupational family, so a single weight hides the only composition that moves it. This would not help for occupations inside that family, where the exclusion leaves nothing to compare, and it is not a substitute for a within-occupation contrast.

**Anyone quoting a published automation share as a model input should state which average it is, and put the wage-weighted version beside it.** The two differ by less than a point in each of these windows, which is a result worth having in print rather than assumed. It would not close the gap for a model that weights tasks by labour payments: the correction measured here re-weights an hourly rate, no hours enter it, and the hours a task takes are not in the released facets for the August window at all.

## Limitations

The design's virtue is that it observes how work was actually done, at task resolution, on more than eight hundred thousand classified conversations a window. Its costs follow from the same choice, and a referee would reach for them in this order.

1. **Composition, not price — and this withdraws a claim.** The top quartile is two thirds to three quarters Computer & Mathematical by usage, and the difference reverses without it, within occupational groups, and on the ten-largest leave-out. The wage attached to a task is the wage of the occupation the task resembles, so the design cannot separate the wage of the work from the kind of work at any grain. The post is therefore not entitled to the sentence "delegation depends on the wage of the work", in either direction, and does not write it. Direction of the bias: unsigned — the confound could as easily be masking a price relation as manufacturing one.

2. **The country mix inside a task.** A task's global rate is a usage-weighted blend over countries and the intersections are published at global grain only, so the blend cannot be cleaned and a task's rate cannot be rebuilt from the country files. Anthropic's own country regression reports lower-usage countries delegating more; if top-quartile tasks are drawn relatively more from high-usage countries — which the global grain cannot confirm — that would push the top quartile's share *down*, so the likelier direction of this bias is against the declared sign. One case can be bounded: Seychelles in November, worth up to three points on the difference, not enough to account for November's excess.

3. **Between-window instability of the size.** Two thirds of a point in one window, more than seven in another, against within-window intervals of about three tenths of a point. Those intervals are the wrong uncertainty for any statement that spans windows, and the pre-registered power calculations assume a constant true difference across windows, which these three estimates refute; no probability of the observed outcome under a varying difference has been computed, and none is quoted. Every interval in this post is also a lower bound, because no release carries a user, session or account identifier to cluster on.

4. **The top quartile's effective size, and the threshold at which the conclusion flips.** Its Kish effective number of tasks is 10.6, 8.9 and 16.4; the two largest software-modification tasks are a quarter to two fifths of its mass; and the third quartile boundary sits on a single computer occupation's wage, so sort order places 5 to 7 percentage points of the top quartile's mass. Two flips are already on the table: the fractional quartile rule takes August below one point, and the modal-holder wage rule takes February below zero.

5. **The second wage source is not a second wage source.** BLS-EP is keyed on a later SOC vintage and prices about a tenth of the coding family's mass, so its rebuild of the difference is the Computer & Mathematical exclusion again rather than an independent test. No independent wage source has confirmed the sign, and none is available while the shipped O\*NET file carries 2010 codes.

6. **The use-case rival is under-tested.** It is testable in two windows, sits exactly on the half-retention boundary in one of them, and cannot be tested in August at all. The composition rule's power against a leg that loses three quarters of the difference is low in this range, so a leg that does not fire is nothing shown; the use-case mix has not been ruled out, and the work-dominant restriction is not independent of the coding exclusion.

7. **The construct.** "Delegated" is `directive` plus `feedback loop`, and in the exploratory split the two components move in opposite directions across the wage distribution, so the positive difference is a feedback-loop difference rather than a difference in complete hand-off. Automation is not autonomy and is not displaced labour, and the wage-weighting correction re-weights an hourly rate with no hours in it.

8. **The wage level is not Anthropic's.** The usage-weighted wage level built here does not reproduce Anthropic's published task-value series on matched windows, which is why the design is rank-based, why the headline is a quartile contrast rather than a dollar slope, and why no dollar level in this post should be read as Anthropic's.

9. **The generalisation bound.** Treated as a statement about tasks rather than about these windows' conversations, the design resolves nothing below about 14 to 20 percentage points — twice the largest difference reported here and twenty to thirty times the smallest.

## Methodology

**Data.** Three Anthropic Economic Index releases, one window each: `release_2025_09_15` (4–11 August 2025, Claude AI Free and Pro), `release_2026_01_15` (13–20 November 2025) and `release_2026_03_24` (5–12 February 2026, Claude AI Free, Pro and Max). The cut is the `onet_task::collaboration` intersection at `geography = global`, with the same wave's `onet_task_pct` as the usage weight. Tasks are priced through the shipped O\*NET 20.1 task statements to `release_2025_02_10/wage_data.csv`, joined on the full ten-character O\*NET-SOC code, `MedianSalary` filtered above 100 and divided by 2,080 for an hourly rate. BLS Employment Projections is the second wage source, used for rank and sign and never for a level. Analysis set: the 1,802 / 2,075 / 2,188 named global tasks with a wage and at least one classified collaboration cell — 97.15 / 96.23 / 96.52% of named-task usage mass, on 818,673 / 854,432 / 848,716 classified conversations, Kish effective 94 / 83 / 126 tasks; 805 / 1,079 / 1,056 tasks with a wage and no classified cell and 5 / 12 / 12 with a cell and no wage are dropped, never zeroed. The wage is attached on the shipped 2010 O\*NET-SOC codes, the only vintage the wage file joins to; any occupational grouping is taken on the 2019 recode with the 2010 grouping reported beside it.

**Measures, as the brief fixed them.** The outcome is Anthropic's own column: "**automation_pct**: Percentage of classifiable collaboration that is automation-focused (directive, feedback loop patterns)" (`release_2025_09_15/data_documentation.md`), built on the classifier's own definition, "Directive – Human delegates complete task execution to AI with minimal interaction" (Economic Index paper, February 2025, Appendix F.3, p. 25). It is not a count of tasks automated and it is not autonomy — "high automation (directive, minimal back-and-forth) but low AI autonomy" (January 2026, p. 19) — and Anthropic's bounds on what one dialogue's shape can mean travel with it: "users might edit and adjust the response they receive from Claude outside the chat window"; "even automation of simple tasks can serve to enhance human capabilities when embedded within broader human-directed workflows" (February 2025 paper, p. 9). The price of a task is Anthropic's construct too — "the average hourly wage of US workers who perform that task" and, where several occupations do it, "we average their wages weighting by employment and the fraction of time spent on that task" (March 2026, p. 8 and footnote 5, p. 11) — and the quartile convention is Anthropic's: "Wage quartiles are calculated using BLS data, weighted by number of transcripts" (June 2026, Figure 1.3 caption, p. 7). The design is rank-based because the wage *level* does not reproduce: this post reports a quartile contrast and a re-weighting, not a dollar level.

**Models.** D and the composition legs are estimated with the task mix held fixed, on a conversation-level binomial; intervals are two-sided 95% and are lower bounds under within-task dependence, since no clustering unit exists in any release. A design-based variance that resamples tasks is reported beside it as the generalisation bound. Δ_W is Cov_w(wage, p) ÷ E_w[wage]. The slope is a usage-weighted regression of the per-task share on the hourly wage, reported for sign and significance only.

**Stress tests.** Twenty-four robustness entries and sixteen descriptive entries, all pre-registered: two alternative multi-holder wage rules, two alternative quartile allocations, a minimum-conversation floor, two Seychelles cuts, a design-based variance model, a permutation placebo, the second wage source, a fourth task-level window on the unchanged taxonomy, a leave-one-group-out series over all twenty-two major groups, and a ten-largest-tasks leave-out. Every confirmatory quantity is produced twice by two independent code paths; the largest disagreement in D between them is 1.8 × 10⁻¹⁴ points, the bootstrap standard errors are within 0.9% of the closed form, and their intervals cover 95.2–95.3%.

**Reproduction of the published numbers first.** Each wave's own five-pattern automation share reproduces at 51.07 / 46.74 / 45.55% (published 49 / 45 / 44% on the all-conversation base), and Anthropic's released regression returns Figure 2.11 exactly (−3.112, partial R² 0.394, N 111).

## What was set in advance

The pre-registration is `posts/post1/prereg/prereg.md`, committed at `066b761` before any primary test: before D, before Δ_W, before any quartile's automation share, before the continuous slope, before any composition leg, before any robustness re-estimate and before any exploratory test. One script had been run at that point and it loads no data — the power calculation on the standard errors the feasibility work had already published. Nobody had computed or seen D, any quartile automation share, any leg, or the permutation null. What had been seen, and is listed in the pre-registration, was the shape of the quartiles without their outcome: the boundaries, the coding family's share of the top quartile, the use-case mix by quartile, the Kish effective counts and the Seychelles figures.

The confirmatory set was named and counted in advance: **17 confirmatory estimates** — D, Δ_W and the slope in three windows each, leg (a) and leg (b) in three each, leg (e) in two — **of which 8 are the leg tests**, plus **3 exploratory tests** labelled as such. Seventeen and three are what was run; nothing else was run as a test.

The assumed effect and its provenance: a smallest effect of interest of one percentage point on D, chosen so that the rule could fail, with a realised minimum detectable effect of 0.40 / 0.39 / 0.39 points per window at 80% power. The separate materiality line for Δ_W — 0.11 to 0.12 points of Δ_W per point of quartile gap — came from the referee's arithmetic on the brief and is reported here as an assumption, not as a realised relation: Δ_W depends on all four quartiles, and the arithmetic holds in one window of three.

Three deviations were logged, each with the registered rule and the corrected rule both run and both reported.

- **The quartile rule at the $43.40 wage mass point.** The registered sentence does not say which of the tasks sharing the boundary wage fall in the top quartile; the registered rule was made reproducible by fixing the tie order on (wage, task text), an order-free fractional rule was run beside it, and both declare the same owner.
- **The two-percent agreement tolerance on the retained fraction.** A ratio with a denominator of two thirds of a point is heavy-tailed and no implementation could meet it; twenty-five percent is asserted for the ratio, and the half-retention judgement is read from the linear contrast the pre-registration already required beside every leg, which does meet two percent.
- **The permutation placebo's size band**, widened from plus or minus one to plus or minus two percentage points, with the Monte Carlo error printed beside the estimate; the realised size falls inside the original band, so the widening did no work. The placebo is in no decision rule.

Three places where the pre-registration completed a rule the brief left open — the persistence of the composition rule, the identified set for the within-group leg, and the declared-power figure for the null — are marked in the document, with the literal reading reported beside the completed one.

## Reproduction

The analysis runs as eight numbered scripts, in order, each ending in a check block that stops on a wrong number: `02_build.py` (frames, joins, merge audits, analysis set, quartiles, replication assertions), `03_headline.py` (D, Δ_W, the slope, intervals, MDEs), `04_second_implementation.py` (the second code path for every confirmatory quantity, the parametric bootstrap, the synthetic recoveries), `05_legs.py` (the composition legs), `06_robustness.py` (the robustness set, the permutation null, the second wage source), `07_fourth_window.py` (the fourth task-level window), `08_exploratory.py` (the exploratory tests) and `09_results_and_figures.py` (`data/processed/results.json`, the four figures and `figures.json`). `01_power_rules.py` precedes them and loads no data.
