## Claims

Web page: references are the section heading or the figure/table number, as the page has no pagination. Numbers are transcribed as published.

1. **India is Claude’s second-largest market by share of use.** "India accounts for 5.8% of total Claude.ai use, second only to the United States." (§Executive summary; restated §India is among the leading countries; Figure 1.) *Comparison: against all other countries, on share of global Claude.ai conversations in the window.*
2. **India ranks 2nd of all countries on total use.** "India ranks second among all countries by share of total Claude.ai use, trailing only the United States." (§India is among the leading countries.) *Comparison: cross-country ranking.*
3. **India ranks 101st on per-capita use.** "on a per-capita basis, adjusting for the working-age population, India ranks 101st out of 116 countries with sufficient observation volume, below other countries in Asia such as Singapore or Malaysia." (§India is among the leading countries.) *Comparison: against 115 other countries clearing the observation threshold, and specifically against Singapore and Malaysia. Rank only — no index value is published for India.*
4. **Global sample size and window.** "Bars show each country’s share of total conversations observed November 13–20, 2025. India highlighted in blue; N = 975, 160 conversations globally." (Figure 1 caption; figure shows the "Top 20 countries by share of global Claude.ai use".) *The stray space in "975, 160" is in the source; Figure 4 gives the same quantity as "975,160".*
5. **Four states hold over half of Indian use.** "Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, and Delhi together account for over half of India’s total Claude.ai use." (§Geographic concentration.) *Comparison: within India, across states.*
6. **State shares.** "Top states: Maharashtra (15.5%), Tamil Nadu (13.2%), Karnataka (12.7%), Delhi (10.5%)." (Figure 2 caption.) *Sums to 51.9% — consistent with the "over half" claim in Claim 5. No other state’s share is published, and no per-state N is given.*
7. **The state pattern tracks the IT sector.** "This pattern closely mirrors India’s IT sector geography and urban economic output." (§Geographic concentration.) *Comparison: against an external benchmark (IT-sector geography, urban economic output) that the brief neither names as a dataset nor quantifies. No correlation, no source.*
8. **The occupational mix skews to software.** "The occupational mix of Indian Claude.ai use, inferred by mapping tasks to related occupations, skews towards software development and engineering roles, consistent with the country’s large IT services sector." (§Concentration of occupational tasks; Figure 3, whose "Orange markers indicate the global average for comparison".) *Comparison: India against the global average, by SOC occupation group.*
9. **India is 1st in the world on software-task share.** "India ranks 1st globally in the share of AI use devoted to software-related tasks (45.2% of all O\*NET-mapped tasks), ahead of Vietnam (42.1%) and Egypt (39.2%)." (§Concentration of occupational tasks; restated in §Implications as "45.2% of tasks map to software-related occupations—the highest share of any country".) *Comparison: cross-country ranking; the two named runners-up are the comparison shown. Denominator is O\*NET-mapped tasks, not all conversations.*
10. **Education and instruction are a second cluster.** "The presence of educational tasks among the most common individual tasks (see Table 1) and when aggregating tasks to occupation groups (see Figure 3) indicates other common use cases in learning and instruction." (§Concentration of occupational tasks; Table 1, "Most common O\*NET tasks among Claude use in India".) *Comparison: within India, across tasks. No number is published for the education share.*
11. **The primitives comparison sample.** "The panels compare India (N = 58,098) against the global average (N = 975,160) across nine economic primitives. November 2025 data." (Figure 4 caption.) *The brief names "nine economic primitives" in the caption but discusses five in the text.*
12. **Greater productivity speedup.** "Indian users take on average 14.8 minutes to complete tasks with AI that would take 3.8 hours without AI—a 15x speedup. Globally, users take on average 15.4 minutes to complete tasks that would take 3.1 hours without AI—a 12x speedup." (§Economic primitives.) *Comparison: India against the global average. Both ratios are consistent with the published inputs as ratios of the two averages (228/14.8 = 15.4; 186/15.4 = 12.1).*
13. **Stronger work orientation.** "51.3% of Indian Claude.ai use is work-related, compared to 46% globally. Coursework accounts for 20.9% (vs. 19.3% globally) and personal use for 27.8% (vs. 34.7% globally)." (§Economic primitives.) *Comparison: India against global. Each triple sums to 100%, so the three categories are exhaustive and mutually exclusive.*
14. **The work-heavy profile matches a main-report finding.** "The work-heavy, lower-personal-use profile is consistent with India’s large professional services sector and the finding from the main report that lower-GDP-per-capita countries tend towards work and coursework over personal use." (§Economic primitives.) *Comparison: against the fourth report’s own cross-country GDP result — the brief’s only explicit link to a prior published finding.*
15. **Higher AI autonomy.** "Indian users delegate more decision-making autonomy to AI (3.60 vs. 3.38 globally on a 1–5 scale, where 1 means no delegation and 5 means extreme delegation)." (§Economic primitives.) *Comparison: India against global mean. Gap = 0.22 scale points; no dispersion or uncertainty published.*
16. **Lower human-only ability.** "We find that 84.6% of tasks could be completed by a human alone (vs. 87.9% globally)". (§Economic primitives.) *Comparison: India against global. Gap = 3.3 percentage points.*
17. **Prompt and response education levels.** "We find that the human education level of prompts (12.2 years) and AI education level of responses (12.5 years) are relatively similar". (§Economic primitives.) *Comparison: the two India measures against each other, not against a global figure — no global value for either is published.*
18. **India is top-decile on AI education level.** "Comparing country averages for AI education, India ranks in the top 10%, indicating Indian users are getting sophisticated outputs from Claude." (§Economic primitives.) *Comparison: cross-country ranking of country averages. Note the tension with Claim 3: India is top-decile on this primitive and 101st of 116 on per-capita adoption.*
19. **Broadening impact requires going beyond software and IT.** "Broadening AI’s economic impact will require looking beyond software and IT services." — resting on the 45.2% software share and the four-state majority. (§Implications, first bullet.) *Comparison: restates Claims 5, 6 and 9.*
20. **India’s per-capita use is what income predicts.** "Globally, per-capita AI adoption is strongly correlated with per-capita income. India’s per-capita use is consistent with what this relationship would predict." (§Implications, third bullet.) *Comparison: against a global income–adoption relationship. No coefficient, no fit statistic, no residual and no chart for this relationship appear on the page; it is attributed to the global data.*
21. **Prompt sophistication correlates with response quality globally.** "The strong correlation between prompt sophistication and response quality in the global data" (§Implications, fifth bullet), glossed earlier as "a global pattern where input quality shapes output quality" (§Economic primitives). *Comparison: a global correlation asserted without a number; India’s 12.2/12.5 pair is offered as mirroring it.*

**Cross-check the wiki author performed on the published numbers (not a claim of the brief):** the India primitives sample, N = 58,098, is 5.96% of the global N = 975,160, whereas the headline share of use is published as 5.8% (which would imply N ≈ 56,559). The two cannot both be shares of the same denominator at the stated precision. The page does not reconcile them, so the 5.8% figure and the Figure 4 N are on different bases (or rounded differently) by some unstated step.

## Data and methods

In the wiki author’s words, with references to the page.

- **Sample.** Claude.ai consumer conversations for 13–20 November 2025 — an eight-day window — described as "privacy-preserving data" and drawn from the fourth report’s sample (§Methodology). Global N = 975,160 conversations (Figures 1 and 4); India N = 58,098 (Figure 4). The Executive summary rounds the global sample to "~1 million". Coverage is Free, Pro and Max plans (§Methodology); the first-party API and Claude Code are not in scope, and no enterprise or team tier is mentioned.
- **Geography.** Country and state are assigned by IP-based geolocation (§Methodology). Country-level rankings require at least 200 observations per country, a rule the page justifies by "the uncertainty of the measure for low-usage countries in our random sample" (§Methodology); 116 countries clear the bar for the per-capita ranking (§India is among the leading countries). Indian states below an unstated threshold are suppressed — "Gray regions indicate insufficient data" (Figure 2). The state map's boundary source is given as "Shapefile for the map from Natural Earth." (Figure 2 caption).
- **Occupation and task.** Conversations are mapped to O\*NET task statements and aggregated to SOC occupation groups (§Methodology); occupation is "inferred by mapping tasks to related occupations" (§Concentration of occupational tasks) — i.e. inferred from the task, not observed from the user. Table 1 ranks individual O\*NET tasks, with "Some task names shortened for readability".
- **Primitives.** Computed by the fourth report’s method, not restated here (§Methodology). The brief’s comparative device throughout is India versus the global average on the same measure, shown as nine panels in Figure 4 and discussed for five primitives in the text: task duration with and without AI, work/coursework/personal split, AI autonomy, human-only ability, and the education-years proxy for prompt and response.
- **Per-capita measure.** Share of Claude.ai use divided by working-age population, reported as a rank among 116 countries. No index value, no denominator vintage and no source for the population data are given on the page. This construct corresponds to what earlier Index releases call the AI Usage Index, but the brief does not name it, so the correspondence is not established by this page.
- **Statistics.** None. There is no regression, no confidence interval, no standard error, no significance test and no sensitivity check anywhere on the page. Every India-versus-global statement is a comparison of two point estimates. The one relationship described as a correlation (income and per-capita adoption, §Implications) is attributed to the global data without a coefficient.
- **Is the country-level cut in the public release?** Not settled from this page, and not assumed here. The fourth release folder on Hugging Face, `release_2026_01_15/`, contains exactly two data files, both raw and both matching this brief’s window: `data/intermediate/aei_raw_claude_ai_2025-11-13_to_2025-11-20.csv` (94.1 MB) and `data/intermediate/aei_raw_1p_api_2025-11-13_to_2025-11-20.csv` (41.5 MB), plus `data_documentation.md` and `aei_v4_appendix.pdf` (per `data/releases/INDEX.md`, enumeration only — the files have not been profiled). So the release that underlies this brief is published and is the right window, but **whether its geography grain reaches country level, and whether it reaches Indian sub-national states, is unconfirmed**; the enumeration did not open the files, and `data/releases/release_2026_01_15.md` does not yet exist. The state-level cut of Figure 2 is the load-bearing question for any post building on this brief, and it must be confirmed by the data steward before use. Earlier releases show that sub-national geography has been published for **US states** (`release_2025_09_15/data/intermediate/gdp_2024_us_state.csv`, `working_age_pop_2024_us_state.csv`), which is not evidence for Indian states.

## What it did not test

The wiki author’s inference, not the brief’s own statements. Each item is something a reader of this page cannot learn from it.

1. **No trend for India.** A single eight-day window. The brief compares India to the global average and to other countries, never to an earlier wave, although geographic data has been published since the third report (Sept 2025). Whether India’s share, its concentration, or any primitive is rising or falling is untested — and the brief points the reader to the parent report for "time-series analysis" rather than doing it for India.
2. **No uncertainty on any gap.** 3.60 vs 3.38 autonomy, 84.6% vs 87.9% human-only ability, 51.3% vs 46% work share, 14.8 vs 15.4 minutes: not one carries an interval or a test. With India at N = 58,098 the India-side precision is likely high, but the brief never says so, and for the *state* and *task* cuts the N is never published at all.
3. **Composition is never held constant.** India’s occupational mix is 45.2% software — the highest in the world — and its autonomy, work-orientation, speedup and human-only-ability gaps are all reported unconditionally. The brief does not ask whether any India-versus-global gap survives conditioning on occupation mix. Every headline primitive difference is consistent with a pure composition effect (software tasks are more work-related, more delegable, longer without help), and the brief’s own causal reading — a distinctively frontier *user base* — is the alternative it does not separate.
4. **The per-capita denominator is not varied.** Working-age population only. Not internet users, not smartphone owners, not English speakers, not the formal-sector workforce. For a rank of 101st out of 116, which of these denominators is used is decisive, and no alternative is shown.
5. **The income–adoption relationship is asserted, not shown.** "strongly correlated" with no coefficient, no scatter, no sample of countries; "consistent with what this relationship would predict" with no fitted value and no residual for India. A reader cannot tell whether India is above or below the line, or by how much.
6. **The "without AI" counterfactual is never validated.** 3.8 hours is an estimated baseline duration, and the brief neither says how it is produced (model inference from the conversation, presumably, by the parent report’s method) nor checks it against any observed completion time, external time-use benchmark or task-level ground truth. The 15x headline rests entirely on it.
7. **The speedup formula is not stated.** 228/14.8 = 15.4 reproduces "15x" as a ratio of two averages; an average of conversation-level ratios would give a different number, likely a much larger one. The brief does not say which it computed, and reports no dispersion, so nothing is known about whether the 15x is typical or driven by a tail.
8. **Two readings of the same number are left unreconciled.** §Economic primitives reads 84.6% as Indian users "more frequently bring tasks to AI that they could not easily accomplish independently"; §Implications lists "frequent use for tasks humans could do alone" among the evidence that autonomy serves Indian users well. Both describe the 84.6% figure. The page does not reconcile them, and a post building on this primitive must decide which reading it is testing.
9. **No within-India heterogeneity.** The primitives are published for India as a single unit. The claim that adoption is driven by "India’s established technology workforce" would be testable by comparing primitives in Maharashtra/Karnataka against low-share states; the brief does not do it. Nor does it report the software-task share by state.
10. **State-level small cells are unquantifiable from the page.** Figure 2 publishes four shares and suppresses the rest as "insufficient data" without stating the threshold or any state N. Whether the fifth-to-tenth states are separable from noise cannot be assessed.
11. **IP geolocation is not stress-tested.** No discussion of VPNs, corporate or cloud egress, or roaming. This matters unusually much here: India is "the world’s largest exporter of IT services", so offshore work performed in India for foreign clients, and work performed abroad by Indian workers, are on opposite sides of the IP boundary and are never distinguished. No sensitivity check.
12. **The firm side of an IT-services economy is out of sample.** Free, Pro and Max only. The first-party API is in the same public release folder for the same window, and Claude Code is the tool most likely used by the software workforce the brief describes, yet neither is analysed for India. The brief’s conclusion that adoption is confined to the IT workforce is drawn from the consumer channel alone.
13. **"Software-related tasks" is not operationalised.** No crosswalk from O\*NET tasks or SOC groups to the "software-related" category behind 45.2%, 42.1% and 39.2%. The world-first ranking is therefore not reproducible from this page, and the sensitivity of the ranking to where the boundary is drawn (does technical writing count? data analysis?) is untested.
14. **Language is absent.** The education-years proxy for prompts and responses is measured in a country with very large non-English and code-mixed usage, and no breakdown by language appears. Whether 12.2 years reflects skill or English proficiency is not addressed.
15. **Selection against the Indian workforce is never benchmarked.** "Concentration" is measured entirely within Claude’s Indian users. No comparison to India’s actual occupational or state distribution (PLFS, NSSO, census) is made, so the brief cannot say how much of the software skew is India’s economy and how much is Claude’s user base. Its four-state claim mirrors "India’s IT sector geography" by assertion, with no source for that geography.
16. **The headline share and the primitives N are not reconciled.** 5.8% of the global sample would be N ≈ 56,559, but Figure 4 gives India N = 58,098 (5.96%). The page does not explain the difference in base.
17. **No comparison to other country briefs.** Australia (Mar 2026) and Canada (Jul 2026) were published later, so the brief could not have used them; India is compared only to the global average and to ad-hoc named countries (Singapore, Malaysia, Vietnam, Egypt), never to a like-for-like set of peers, and no full country table is published.
18. **No welfare or distributional claim is tested.** "Investing in AI can provide substantial and measurable productivity gains" and "appears to be serving Indian users well" are read off usage measures. No wage, employment, output or firm-performance outcome appears anywhere, and the brief does not test whether any of these usage patterns moves an economic outcome.
