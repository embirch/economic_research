## Claims

**Adoption level and rank**

1. (§Key Findings, bullet 1; §High adoption, ¶1; Figure 1) "Australia is among the leading adopters of Claude, accounting for 1.6% of global Claude.ai traffic." Comparison: Australia's share of the 1M-conversation February 2026 Claude.ai sample against all other countries in the sample. Published rank: "ranking eleventh among all countries in our February 2026 sample".
2. (§Key Findings, bullet 1; §High adoption, ¶1) "Per capita, Australians' use of Claude is more than four times higher than expected for the size of its population." Comparison: Australia's share of global Claude.ai usage against Australia's share of the world's working-age population (the AUI construct, parity = 1.0).
3. (§High adoption, ¶1) Australia ranks "seventh behind Singapore, Israel, Luxembourg, Switzerland, the United States, and Canada" on per capita adoption. Comparison: AUI ranking across countries. (Six countries are named for a seventh-place rank, which is internally consistent.)

## Data and methods

In the wiki author's own words, with references to the page.

- **Universe.** Claude.ai consumer traffic only. The page speaks throughout of "Claude.ai traffic" (§Key Findings, §High adoption) and of conversations "sampled from Claude.ai" (Figure 1 caption). No first-party API cut, no Claude Code cut, and no enterprise or Team/Max-tier split is reported for Australia anywhere on the page.
- **Sample.** "the 1M conversations sampled from Claude.ai in February 2026" (Figure 1 caption); "our February 2026 sample" (§High adoption, ¶1). Country and state figures are shares of that sample. The page gives no date range within February, no sampling design, no weighting and no confidence intervals.
- **Geography.** Country assignment is unexplained on the page; Anthropic's public data documentation for the matching release assigns geography by IP-based geolocation to ISO 3166-1 country codes and ISO 3166-2 subnational regions. The eight Australian states and territories correspond to ISO 3166-2 codes `AU-NSW`, `AU-VIC`, `AU-QLD`, `AU-WA`, `AU-SA`, `AU-ACT`, `AU-TAS`, `AU-NT`.
- **AUI.** Two different AUIs appear and the page does not flag the difference. The country AUI of 4.1 (claim 2) benchmarks Australia's share of *global* Claude.ai usage against its share of the *world's* working-age population. The state AUIs of 1.20, 1.19, 0.68, 0.32, 0.12 (claim 6) benchmark each state's share of *Australian* usage against its share of *Australia's* working-age population, using ABS Estimated Resident Population, June quarter 2025 (Figure 3 caption). A state AUI of 1.0 therefore means "proportional within Australia", not "proportional globally".
- **Income measure.** Gross state product per capita from ABS Australian National Accounts: State Accounts, 2024–25, expressed relative to the Australian average of $101,438 (Figure 3 caption). The income–adoption comparison is a scatter of eight points read by eye; the page reports no correlation coefficient, no regression and no test statistic.
- **Primitives.** Use-case mix, AI autonomy, task success and task complexity are taken from the framework of the January 2026 report ("Our fourth Economic Index report introduced four economic primitives", §Anglosphere peers, ¶1). This page places Australia within those measures; it does not re-derive or re-validate them. Peer groups are defined by two thresholds (Figure 4 caption): at least 200 sampled Claude.ai conversations, and AUI above 1 for the "high-adoption" grey group.
- **Task and occupation classification.** Conversations are mapped to O\*NET tasks and aggregated to SOC major groups (§Broader task mix, ¶¶1–2). Diversity is measured as the share of a geography's classified usage falling in that geography's own top 100 tasks; composition is measured as percentage-point differences between Australia's top-100 distribution and the global top-100 distribution by SOC major group. Note the asymmetry the page itself records: the Australian top 100 is drawn from 171 distinct tasks observed, the global top 100 from 3,258 (Figure 6 caption), so the two "top 100" baskets are not built from comparable task counts.
- **Cross-report borrowings.** Three results are imported rather than estimated here: the cross-country income–usage association (September 2025 report), the income–use-case relationships and the adoption–complexity relationships (January 2026 report), and the US-state pattern invoked in the Conclusion.
- **Is the underlying state-level cut in the public release? Yes.** The Economic Index public release `release_2026_03_24` on Hugging Face (`Anthropic/EconomicIndex`) carries a `country-state` geography level with ISO 3166-2 identifiers globally, and all eight Australian codes appear in `release_2026_03_24/data/aei_raw_claude_ai_2026-02-05_to_2026-02-12.csv`. The `usage_pct` values for the eight reproduce Figure 2 exactly (NSW 37.162, VIC 30.850, QLD 17.673, WA 7.582, SA 4.571, ACT 1.408, TAS 0.629, NT 0.126), and the underlying `usage_count` values sum to 15,906 Australian conversations — consistent with 1.6% of a 1M-conversation sample. The facets present for Australian states in that file are `request`, `onet_task`, `collaboration`, `use_case`, `task_success`, `multitasking` and `human_only_ability`. **What is not in the release:** AUI itself (a derived quantity requiring a working-age-population denominator, which the release does not ship for non-US subnational geographies), GSP per capita, and the Anglosphere peer-group construction. The data steward owns the authoritative profile of this release; the checks above are the wiki author's, recorded in Verification, and should be confirmed against `data/releases/release_2026_03_24.md` when that file exists.

2. §High adoption, ¶4: "This likely reflects these sparsely populated states and territories having a relatively smaller share of workers in occupations where Claude tends to be adopted."

## What it did not test

The wiki author's inference, not the page's words. Each item is something the page asserts, implies or leaves open but does not establish with a number on the page.

1. **The composition conjecture is never measured.** Items 1, 2 and 4 in the section above attribute the state pattern to workforce composition, but no occupational or industry shares for the eight states enter any figure. No state-level employment data (ABS industry of employment, or the occupational mix of each state) is joined to state AUI. The claim that adoption "tracks workforce composition rather than income" (Conclusion) is asserted from four illustrative cases, not tested.
2. **The public-sector conjecture for the ACT.** No measure of public-sector employment share, procurement policy, or institutional access to Claude is brought to bear.
3. **No statistical test of the income–AUI relationship.** Eight points, no correlation coefficient, no slope, no standard error, and — given the page's own "suggestive rather than conclusive" — no statement of what effect size eight states could have detected.
4. **No cell sizes or uncertainty for the state estimates.** Figure 2 reports the Northern Territory at 0.1% and Tasmania at 0.6% of Australian usage without saying how many conversations that is. (In the matching public release the counts are 20 and 100 respectively.) No confidence interval, bootstrap or noise check appears for any state number, including the AUIs built on them.
5. **No decomposition of diversity into adoption level.** The page notes that "part of Australia's lower coding share is expected" from higher adoption, but never partials adoption out: it does not report Australia's residual diversity after conditioning on AUI, nor the slope of top-100 concentration on adoption across countries.
6. **No within-Australia task or use-case mix.** Everything below the national level is a volume share. Whether NSW's mix differs from Queensland's — the state-level equivalent of the whole second half of the page — is not shown, although the underlying facets exist for Australian states in the public release.
7. **No time dimension.** One February 2026 snapshot. No trend in Australia's share, AUI, autonomy or coding share across the six Economic Index waves; no before/after around the Sydney office or the MOU that occasioned the piece.
8. **No API, Claude Code or enterprise cut for Australia.** The first-party API data in the matching release carries no geographic breakdown at all, so "how Australia uses Claude" is necessarily consumer-only; the page does not say so.
9. **No collaboration/automation-vs-augmentation split for Australia**, even though the `collaboration` facet exists for Australian states in the public release, and even though the autonomy finding is glossed in collaboration language ("more collaborative, less delegated ways").
10. **No task-success or multitasking result for Australia**, although task success is one of the four primitives the page introduces and the facet is present in the release.
11. **The Anglosphere average is not defined numerically.** Claim 21 cites an "Anglosphere average of 8.9pp" and peer averages of +1.2pp, +0.1pp and +1.6pp, but the page never says which countries enter that average (Figure 4's Anglosphere list includes New Zealand and Ireland, which Figure 5 drops for insufficient distinct tasks) or whether it is weighted.
12. **No measurement-validity work.** IP geolocation, VPN use, tourists and expatriates, multi-account users and the residency of Claude subscribers are not discussed; nor is the mapping from an Australian conversation to an Australian worker. Occupation in the Index is inferred from the task, not the user.
13. **No denominator sensitivity.** AUI uses working-age (15–64) population; the page does not show whether the state ordering survives alternative denominators (total population, employed persons, internet users, or the professional workforce that the composition conjecture itself invokes).
14. **The "sales" offset is never shown.** The key findings and the Conclusion both name sales among the offsetting categories; no figure on the page reports a sales number.
15. **No price, availability or product confound.** Subscription pricing in AUD, timing of feature availability in the Asia-Pacific region, and the Sydney office launch are not examined as sources of the level or the trend.
