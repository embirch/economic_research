## Data and methods

What the page states, in the wiki author's words, with references. The page is an announcement, so most of this section is a record of design commitments and of what is absent.

- **Cadence.** Monthly, from April 22, 2026 (*Lead ¶1*, *Lead ¶6*, meta description). No end date, no statement of whether every calendar month is fielded, no field-window length within a month, and no statement of when the first wave closed.
- **Sampling frame.** Claude users with a personal account at least two weeks old; invitations go to "a small, randomly selected group" each month (*Lead ¶6*, *FAQ Q1*). The frame is therefore the Claude consumer user base, not a population; the linked privacy article adds that Anthropic Interviewer runs on "Claude Free, Pro, Max" and "is not currently available for our commercial products such as Claude for Work and the Anthropic API" (definition 13). No sample size, no invited-sample size, no response or completion rate, no stratification, no weighting scheme, no geographic or language coverage, no minimum-cell or privacy threshold, and no statement of the unit of observation are given anywhere on the page.
- **Rotation, and whether this is a panel.** The announcement commits to rotation — "We will rotate the sample each month", "We invite a new set of Claude users each month" (*Lead ¶6*, *FAQ Q1*) — i.e. repeated cross-sections rather than a panel. The privacy documentation leaves a partial panel open: "if you participate in more than one Anthropic Interviewer study, we may analyze your responses together to understand how perspectives change over time" (definition 17). The two are not reconciled in either document; whether rotation is with or without replacement, and whether a rotating-panel overlap is designed in, is not stated.
- **Instrument.** Anthropic Interviewer: a Claude-conducted conversational interview in the participant's own language, from a researcher-finalised interview guide that Claude drafts, with the tool also performing thematic analysis (definitions 12, 14). The announcement gives the topic list twice (definitions 4, 8) and **no question wording, no response scales, no ordering, no branching rules and no interview length**. **No instrument document is published with this announcement** — no questionnaire PDF, no codebook, no pre-registration.
- **Mode and recruitment.** In-product invitation: a banner in claude.ai, the Cowork desktop app, or email for mobile-only (or "primarily" mobile) users (*Lead ¶6*, *FAQ Q1*; note the discrepancy recorded in claim 15). Participation is voluntary ("We'd love you to take part"), and each participant is informed of intended data use before agreeing (definition 14). No incentive is mentioned.
- **Linkage to usage data.** Promised in principle, with the method named only by link: "Combined with Claude usage data in a [privacy-preserving way](https://www.anthropic.com/research/clio)" (*Lead ¶5*), i.e. the Clio / Anthropic Insights system (wiki slug `clio-insights-2024-12`). The operative privacy article describes it as combining "your interview responses with aggregated, privacy-preserving summaries of your Claude usage data" using automated techniques "that group related responses together" (definition 17). No sampling rule, window, session cap or exclusion rule for the usage side is stated in any of these documents. *(Cross-reference, marked: the actual linkage procedure was first published two months later in the sixth Economic Index report — up to 20 randomly sampled sessions per person in a mid-May to early-June window across Claude.ai, Cowork and Claude Code, respondents with fewer than five sessions excluded, about 9,700 linked respondents. That is a statement of that report, recorded in `wiki/reports/economic-index-2026-06-report.md` (p.19), not of this announcement.)*
- **Release plans, as stated.** Insights "in future Anthropic Economic Index reports and other research briefs" (*Lead ¶6*); findings published, with de-identified responses possibly included for users who opt in (*FAQ Q3*). **No public microdata, dataset, Hugging Face release or replication package is promised anywhere on this page**, and no schedule is given. The wider Interviewer documentation goes further than the announcement in one respect — sharing Study Participation Data "with select third parties such as external researchers or journalists" (definition 18) — which the announcement does not mention.
- **Legal and privacy basis.** Processing under the Supplemental Anthropic Interviewer Privacy Policy (*FAQ Q3*), whose legal bases for research use are listed as consent where requested, scientific research, and legitimate interests, separately for research excluding and including model training (Supplemental Privacy Policy, "Legal Bases for Processing Study Participation Data"). Recorded because it bears on what a later post may assume about the population that consents.
- **Relation to the rest of the corpus, as the page itself states it.** This survey is positioned as the continuation of the December Anthropic Interviewer wave analysed in the companion economics report (*Lead ¶4*), and its outputs are promised to the Economic Index report series (*Lead ¶6*). The announcement itself contributes no measurement.

## Open questions, conjectures and promised follow-ups (verbatim)

Every forward-looking statement on the page, plus the two in the linked documents that bear on research use. Each carries a status line: whether a later publication answered it, as far as the corpus catalogued on 2026-09-16 shows. Status lines are the wiki author's, and cross-references to later publications are to other wiki files, which are authoritative for their own sources.

1. *Lead ¶5* — **the monthly-cadence promise**: "Collecting these data monthly will enable measurement of not just what people experience and expect, but how quickly their views shift as AI capabilities evolve." **Status: open.** This requires at least two waves to be compared. The sixth Economic Index report (2026-06-26) publishes first-wave results only; no wave-over-wave change in survey responses appears in the corpus as catalogued on 2026-09-16. To be carried into `programme/LEDGER.md` as an unanswered promise, and re-checked whenever a seventh report appears.
2. *Lead ¶5* — **the lead-indicator conjecture**: "Combined with Claude usage data in a [privacy-preserving way](https://www.anthropic.com/research/clio), these first-hand accounts can surface change before it shows up in aggregate labor market data." **Status: asserted, untested.** No published Anthropic work tests the timing claim — i.e. compares a survey-plus-usage signal against a later movement in an aggregate labour series. The sixth report links survey responses to usage and benchmarks levels against BLS series; that is a contemporaneous comparison, not a lead-lag test. This is the single most load-bearing untested conjecture on the page.
3. *Lead ¶3* — **the forecasting rationale**: "To forecast a transition that is still unfolding, we need to hear from the people who are living through it, and we need to do so on a cadence that can identify changes as they emerge." **Status: open.** Whether respondents' expectations have any forecast accuracy is not addressed here and no accuracy test appears later in the corpus; the sixth report reports anticipated exposure at a 12-month horizon, whose realisation cannot be checked before 2027.
4. *Lead ¶6* — **the publication promise**: "We plan to publish insights in future Anthropic Economic Index reports and other research briefs." **Status: kept, in part.** First survey results were published in chapter 3 of the sixth Economic Index report, 2026-06-26 (see `wiki/reports/economic-index-2026-06-report.md`), which states there: "In April 2026, we launched the Anthropic Economic Index Survey to build on this work." No separate research brief built on this survey appears in the corpus as catalogued on 2026-09-16.
5. *Lead ¶6* — **the breadth promise**: "We will rotate the sample each month so that we hear from as broad a range of people as possible over time." **Status: open.** No published statement of achieved breadth — cumulative distinct respondents, coverage of countries, languages, occupations or account tiers — exists in the corpus. The sixth report instead documents the opposite of breadth in one dimension: occupational over-representation of Computer and Mathematical occupations among respondents.
6. *FAQ Q2* — **the ten-year hopes question**: "how you hope an economy shaped by AI will look in ten years." **Status: answered once.** The sixth report's chapter 3 reports classifier-tagged themes from a "dream big" closing question at a ten-year horizon; whether that is the same item is not stated in either document.
7. *FAQ Q1* — **the recurring-invitation promise to users**: "We invite a new set of Claude users each month, so if you're not selected this time, keep an eye out." **Status: unverifiable from published sources.**

## What it did not test

**This section is the wiki author's inference, not the page's text.** An announcement tests nothing, so the items below record (a) the design questions the announcement leaves open that a later post could exploit, and (b) framings the page uses without support. Items answered by the sixth report are marked; `wiki/reports/economic-index-2026-06-report.md` is authoritative on that report.

*Design questions left open*

1. **No instrument in public.** The topic list is given twice and the question wording never. Without wording, response scales or ordering, no external researcher can assess acquiescence, framing or anchoring in any published survey number. **Corrected 2026-09-16** (`room/referee-2026-09-16-ledger-threads-verdict.md`, correction 1): this item previously said the sixth report's appendix publishes the instrument. **It does not.** `economic-index-2026-06-appendix` chapter 3 is Table A.1 and its caption only — the ten-year vision themes and their definitions — and the caption is a paraphrase of one question, not its wording (re-fetched and re-read at PDF p.15 on 2026-09-16; `wiki/reports/economic-index-2026-06-appendix.md` says so four times). What the corpus does print, all of it in the report body and none of it an instrument: one question verbatim, "dream big: what do you hope an economy shaped by AI looks like in ten years?" (`economic-index-2026-06-report` p.30); the exposure items' response format, as bands whose midpoints are taken (ibid., Figure 3.3 caption, p.23); and paraphrases of the experience and job-loss items (ibid., ch.3 footnotes 7 and 11, p.31). No full wording, no scales, no ordering, no branching rules exist in public for any wave.
2. **No response rate and no invited-sample count.** "A small, randomly selected group" is the only quantity. Random invitation plus voluntary completion is not a random sample of the frame, and nothing on the page acknowledges the gap between the two.
3. **Non-response is measurable here and is not measured.** The linkage infrastructure means Anthropic can observe usage for invited non-respondents as well as respondents. A respondent-versus-invited-non-respondent comparison on observable usage is therefore available and is promised nowhere. This is the sharpest gap on the page.
4. **Rotation versus panel, and what the headline promise needs.** Claim 11 promises to measure "how quickly their views shift". A rotating cross-section can only show how the *aggregate* shifts; within-person belief revision needs repeat respondents. The privacy documentation contemplates repeat participation but the announcement's design commitment is rotation, and no overlap fraction is stated. Any post using two waves must treat composition change as the first rival explanation.
5. **The two-week account-age rule selects out the newest users.** People in their first fortnight with Claude are precisely those whose expectations are being formed, and they are excluded. Neither the size nor the direction of that exclusion is discussed.
6. **The frame excludes the commercial surfaces the Index itself measures.** Interviewer runs on Free, Pro and Max; Claude for Work and the API are out. The Economic Index reports first-party API usage as a separate population with markedly different automation behaviour, and no survey reaches it. The announcement does not say so.
7. **Interviewer effects are not addressed.** The interview is conducted by Claude, inside the Claude product, about AI's effect on the respondent's work, following an invitation banner from the vendor. Demand effects, acquiescence, and the selection of people willing to be interviewed *by an AI about AI* are all plausible and all unmentioned. No split-sample, no human-interviewer arm, no alternative-mode comparison is proposed.
8. **Claude also codes the answers.** The instrument's third stage has the tool synthesise responses and identify themes (definition 14). Nothing on this page or in the linked documents describes validation of that coding — no human double-coding, no inter-rater agreement, no sensitivity to prompt wording.
9. **No benchmark against a probability sample is promised.** The obvious external validity check is to field the same items on a nationally representative panel and compare. The announcement does not mention one. (The sixth report does cite a nationally representative comparator for corroboration; the announcement does not commit to any.)
10. **"Real-time data" (meta description) is not defined and not bounded.** No latency from field close to publication is stated; the first published results arrived about two months after the first wave.
11. **No pre-registration, no hypotheses, no analysis plan** — for a monthly instrument whose stated purpose is to detect change, with no stated rule for what counts as a change.
12. **No microdata commitment.** Publication is promised as "insights" and opt-in de-identified responses. Nothing commits aggregate survey tabulations to the Economic Index public release, and as of the June 2026 release the survey is not in the released data. *(That statement is recorded in `wiki/reports/economic-index-2026-06-report.md`; the data steward's `data/releases/` files are authoritative and a room note should confirm it before any post relies on it.)*
13. **Language and country coverage unstated.** Interviewer conducts interviews "in the participant's own language" and the December wave ran in 70 languages, but this announcement commits to nothing about coverage, so cross-country use of any survey number rests on unpublished composition.
14. **No incentive, and no statement about what participation costs the respondent.** Interview length is never given, which bears directly on who finishes.

*Framings used without support on this page*

15. **"complementing traditional labor statistics" (meta description) and "before it shows up in aggregate labor market data" (Lead ¶5).** Both assert a timing relationship to official statistics with no series named, no lag estimated and no test. The page's own framing of official data — "track what has already happened, often with meaningful delay" — quantifies neither the delay nor the proposed improvement on it.
16. **"a rich new corpus of qualitative data" (Lead ¶4) presented alongside a promise of "measurement" (Lead ¶5).** The page slides between qualitative interviewing and quantitative measurement of shifts without saying how open-ended answers become a measure; the classifier step is in the About page, not here.
17. **"how people experience the changes to the economy that AI brings" (Lead ¶2).** Respondents are asked about their own work and their own organisation; "the changes to the economy" is a much larger object than the instrument's reach, and the gap is not flagged.
18. **"the people who are living through it" (Lead ¶3).** The people surveyed are Claude users on consumer plans. Those most exposed to displacement may be the least likely to be paying Claude subscribers, and the page treats the user base as a window on the transition without qualification.
19. **The invitation-channel discrepancy between the lead and the FAQ** (claim 15: Cowork named only in the FAQ; "primarily" versus "only" on mobile). Small in itself, but it means the announced frame is not stated consistently within one page.
20. **"Economic Research team" (Lead ¶1)** is not the name used on the team page it links to ("The Economics team"). Recorded so that no later post attributes the survey to a team name Anthropic does not use for itself.
