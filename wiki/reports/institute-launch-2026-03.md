## What it did not test

**This section is the wiki author's inference, not the page's text.** An announcement tests nothing, so the items below record (a) what a later post can exploit in the gap between what the Institute promised and what the corpus delivered, and (b) framings this page uses without support. Cross-references are by slug; each cross-referenced wiki file is authoritative for its own source, and files marked *(not yet written)* did not exist on 2026-09-16.

*The five-into-four collapse, and what the economics label does*

1. **Five questions on the launch page become four problem areas on the landing page, and the merge is undocumented.** *¶3* asks separately about resilience opportunities and about threats; definition 17 merges them into "Threats and resilience." The economics question survives alone as its own area. Nobody states the mapping, and the labels then change again in `institute-agenda-2026-05` ("Economic diffusion"). **Consequence for our programme:** there is no single canonical statement of what the Institute means by its economics remit, and a post that says "Anthropic names AI, jobs and the economy as one of four problem areas" must cite the landing page, not the launch note, and must accept the dating limitation in `## Limitations (verbatim)` item 6.
2. **"AI, jobs, and the economy" is a problem area with no measurement definition attached anywhere.** The landing page's two questions (definition 16) are "How is AI reshaping jobs, inside Anthropic and elsewhere? And how do these impacts add up for economies around the world?" The second is an aggregation question — how impacts *add up* — and the only Anthropic attempt at aggregation in the corpus is `productivity-gains-2025-11` (Hulten aggregation of self-reported time savings) and `econ-scenarios-paper-2026-09` (a calibrated model). Neither aggregates measured Claude usage into a measured economy-wide impact. **The aggregation question the Institute sets itself is open.**
3. **"inside Anthropic and elsewhere" (definition 16) pairs an n=1 firm study with global usage data, and nothing reconciles them.** `work-at-anthropic-2025-12` is the inside; the Index is the elsewhere. No publication in the corpus tests whether Anthropic's internal experience generalises, and the launch post's own *¶1* uses the internal result ("accelerate the pace of AI development itself") as evidence about AI in general.

*Framings used without support on this page*

4. **The Economic Index is cited as evidence of capability.** In *¶1* the words "take on a wide range of real work" link to the Index hub and to the primitives blog. **The Index measures usage, not capability**, and Anthropic says so elsewhere: `economic-index-connector-2026-07` states that "the Index reflects patterns in Claude usage rather than the labor market as a whole" (recorded in `wiki/reports/programme-and-product-pages.md`). Using usage breadth as a capability claim is exactly the construct slide our own posts must avoid, and it is being made by the Institute's founding document. A strong candidate for a stated-interest-versus-own-caveat tension in `programme/THREADS.md`.
5. **"tracks its impact on jobs and the larger economy" (*¶5*) describes the Economic Research team as measuring impact.** As of this date the Index measured usage; the first measure aiming at impact, observed exposure, arrived six days earlier in `labor-market-impacts-2026-03` and is explicitly a measure of exposure, not of realised employment effects. The remit as written outruns the published measurement, and the page does not flag the gap.
6. **"information that only the builders of frontier AI systems possess" (*¶6*) is asserted, never inventoried.** No document in the corpus lists what Anthropic can see and does not publish. This matters directly to us: every claim we make about what the released data *cannot* show rests on the steward's reading of the files, not on any Anthropic statement of what was held back. (`independent-research-access-2026-08` is the only later document that moves on this; it is authoritative on its own scope.)
7. **"the people and communities who feel the future bearing down on them" (*¶6*) is a population the corpus never samples.** Every survey instrument in the corpus draws from Claude users (`survey-81k-interviews-2026-03`, `economic-index-survey-2026-04-announcement`) or a general US panel (`econ-scenarios-explorer-2026-09`). Selection into Claude subscription is plausibly *negatively* related to displacement risk. Nothing on this page, and nothing in the later survey documents, acknowledges that the promised population and the sampled population differ.
8. **A conflict of interest is structurally present and nowhere declared.** The same organisation builds the technology, measures its economic effects, and (in the adjacent half of this very page) advocates policy on them. The post announces the separation of Institute and Public Policy but states no firewall, no independence mechanism, no external review and no pre-registration. Anthropic's later documents partially address this — external reviewers are thanked on the scenario explorer, the LTBT link appears in `institute-agenda-2026-05` — but the founding document declares nothing.
9. **"reporting candidly" (*¶6*) has no criterion and no audit.** No document states a negative-result policy. For our purposes: the corpus contains no Anthropic publication whose headline finding is that AI's measured effect was smaller than expected, which is worth checking rather than asserting, and is a fair thing for an external post to check on the released data.
10. **The prediction in *¶2* ("the next two years") is unquantified and therefore cannot be scored.** Anthropic's own later scenario work is explicit that outcomes depend on capability *and* adoption, and the scenario explorer's disclaimer concedes it "is a stark simplification". The launch note's framing has no such hedge. A post that wants to test Anthropic against itself should test the scenario paper's parameters, not this sentence.

*Gaps left open that our programme can exploit*

11. **No stated success criteria for the Institute's own research.** Nothing says what evidence would change the Institute's mind about any of the five questions. This is an argument for our briefs stating counter-evidence for every hypothesis, per `team/agents/programme-lead.md`; it is also a genuine contribution available to an external post: name the criterion Anthropic did not.
12. **The economics remit names jobs and economies; the released data names tasks and conversations.** The whole distance between the Institute's stated problem and its published evidence is the mapping from conversation-level task shares to labour-market quantities. `labor-market-impacts-2026-03` builds one bridge (capability x usage x automation weighting) and `labor-market-impacts-2026-03-appendix` formalises it. No publication in the corpus tests that bridge against a second, independent construction. **The most promising Institute-level gap for a post.**
13. **The two-way street was never measured in the direction it promised.** *¶6* promises that what Anthropic learns from workers will change what it studies. A post could ask the answerable version on public data: do the topics the Index reports over time move toward the concerns the 81k respondents raised, or not? Either answer is a finding. Flagged to `programme/LONGLIST.md` for session 1.2.
