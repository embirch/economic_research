## Limitations (verbatim)

**The page states no limitation in the research sense.** There is no limitations section, no caveat about what inside-the-lab data cannot show, no representativeness statement, no uncertainty quantification and no statement of what the Institute will be unable to answer. The complete set of qualifying, hedging or bounding statements on the page is the following eight, quoted verbatim.

1. *Lead ¶9* — the agenda binds nothing, and is incomplete by its own account: "This is a living agenda, rather than a fixed one. We'll continue to fine-tune these questions as evidence accumulates, and we expect new questions to emerge that aren't captured here today."
2. *Lead ¶4* — the sharing commitment is explicitly bounded, without saying by what: "In order to realize the full benefits of AI progress, we want to share as much of that information as we can."
3. *Lead ¶4* — the evidence behind the page's central labour-market assertion is characterised as early: "we can see early evidence that jobs like software engineering are changing radically"; and, in the same sentence, "early signs of AI contributing to speeding up the research and development of AI itself".
4. *Share 1* — the early-warning commitment is hedged to an attempt: "We'll try to be an early warning signal for significant change and disruption."
5. *Lead ¶7* — the influence claim is hedged to a possibility: "That may look like the company sharing data with the world that it otherwise would not (like the Economic Index), or approaching how it releases technology differently".
6. *Lead ¶8* — the LTBT relationship is expected, not established: "We expect that work developed by The Anthropic Institute will increasingly serve as important inputs to Anthropic's Long-Term Benefit Trust (LTBT)."
7. *RD intro ¶1*–*¶2* — the central R&D mechanism is hedged twice: "increasingly powerful systems may be used to help develop successor versions of themselves"; "AI-driven AI R&D may be a 'natural dividend' of making smarter and more capable systems."
8. *TR-5* — a question the page allows may have no answer, the only place it concedes that one of its own research aims may be unattainable: "Or is the asymmetry structural?"

10. **`ED-1` "Who adopts AI?"** — "AI development is concentrated in a small number of companies in a small number of countries, but deployment is global. What would a tabletop exercise look like that actually tests the decision-making of lab leadership, boards, and governments?" **Status: outside this corpus. Note the term's economic reuse: `economic-futures-research-fund-2026-07` describes a "fire drill" design for scaling an existing programme rapidly in one state (recorded in `wiki/reports/programme-and-product-pages.md`), and `econ-scenarios-explorer-2026-09` is the corpus's scenario instrument.**
41. **`RD-3` "Telemetry for AI R&D:"** — "How can we measure the aggregate speed of AI research and development? What sorts of telemetry and underlying technical affordances must exist in order to gather this information? How might metrics relating to AI R&D serve as early warning signals for recursive self-improvement?" **Status: open; the corpus's only measurement of research speed-up in a real organisation is `work-at-anthropic-2025-12`, and `coding-agents-social-sciences-2026-05` measures research-output differences among social scientists using coding agents. Neither is aggregate and neither is framed as telemetry.**
42. **`RD-4` "Controlling AI acceleration:"** — "If an intelligence explosion was upon us, what intervention points would facilitate slowing or otherwise changing the rate of the explosion? Assuming humans can intervene, which entities should wield this capacity—governments? Companies?" **Status: outside this corpus; it is the R&D twin of `ED-8`'s diffusion dials, and the two are nowhere reconciled on the page.**
43. **`RD-5` "The tech tree:"** — "AI is speeding up some sciences far faster than others, depending on data availability, evaluation signals, and how much knowledge is tacit or institutionally gated. How uneven is this gradient, and what does the changing composition of scientific progress imply for which human problems get solved first?" **Status: open, and the most Index-shaped question in the non-economic pillars** — the corpus's occupational and task taxonomies already distinguish research and science work, and `coding-agents-social-sciences-2026-05` measures one field's adoption. Whether the released data can separate sciences is a **steward question**; `data/ATLAS.md` is authoritative.
44. **`RD-6` "The jagged frontier:"** — "Model capabilities are stronger in some domains than in others. Domains with large positive externalities—like drug discovery and materials science—receive less investment than their value warrants. Markets steer the direction of model improvement according to private return, but can we improve how models perform to address social externalities?" **Status: open; the private-versus-social-return frame repeats `ED-3`. The corpus's nearest published construct is the capability-times-usage structure of `labor-market-impacts-2026-03`, which measures where capability and use coincide, not where they should.**

## What it did not test

**This section is the wiki author's inference, not the page's text.** An agenda tests nothing, so the items below record (a) the stated questions that no publication in `wiki/INDEX.md` has yet addressed, cross-referenced by slug, and (b) the framings the page uses without support. Every slug's own file is authoritative for that publication.

*Stated questions with no publication behind them, as of 2026-09-16*

1. **Firm-level economics, in its entirety (`ED-2`).** Causes of adoption, consequences, efficient scale, concentration across firms, markups, labour share, industrial organisation, workplace surveillance. Nothing in the corpus can speak to any of these, and the reason is structural: the unit of observation in every release is a conversation, and `wiki/reports/economic-index-2026-01-blog.md` records the report's own statement that no user- or firm-level analysis is possible. `work-at-anthropic-2025-12` is n = 1 firm and is Anthropic. **The single largest gap between what the Institute says it wants and what it has published.**
2. **What determines access to AI, and how value is captured (`ED-1`).** The corpus measures where Claude is used (AUI, `economic-index-2025-09-report`; the three spotlights) and never why, and never with an outcome. No determinant is estimated; no income, output or trade variable is joined to usage in any Anthropic publication. Free and open-weight models are mentioned nowhere in the corpus.
3. **Whether AI follows the general-purpose-technology pattern (`ED-3`) and whether model improvement tracks private rather than social return (`RD-6`).** Posed twice on the page, addressed nowhere. No corpus publication classifies usage or capability by private against social return.
4. **Diffusion dials (`ED-8`).** No corpus publication measures the response of usage to anything a lab controls — price, rate limits, capability gating, release timing — although release-timing natural experiments exist in the corpus's own history (`economic-index-2025-03-report` re-measures 11 days after a model launch, which is the closest thing to a lever study Anthropic has published, and it was not framed as one).
5. **Transaction costs, exchange and negotiating agents (`ED-6`).** Untouched.
6. **Worker power and workplace surveillance (`ED-9`, `ED-2`).** The corpus asks people how they feel (`survey-81k-*`, `anthropic-interviewer-2025-12`) and never asks about influence, voice or monitoring.
7. **New tasks and jobs that could emerge (`ED-7`).** By construction invisible to a taxonomy of existing O*NET tasks; no corpus publication attempts an emergent-task measure. (The bottom-up 630-cluster taxonomy of `economic-index-2025-03-report` is the only corpus instrument that does not start from O*NET, and it is not used for this purpose. Whether the released data supports an emergent-task cut is a **steward question**.)
8. **The long-run stock of expertise (`ED-10`, `ED-11`).** Every corpus measure is contemporaneous; the question is about a stock decades out. `claude-code-expertise-2026-06` and `economic-index-2026-03-report` measure the gradient today, which is the right first step and not the same question. Professions named by the agenda and absent from the evidence: paralegals, junior analysts.
9. **Time use and the role of paid work (`ED-12`).** No corpus release observes non-users or non-work time, so this question cannot currently be addressed with Anthropic data at all.
10. **The early-warning function (`Share 1`).** Promised, undefined, and untested: no trigger, no threshold, no lead-lag validation anywhere in the corpus. The same untested lead-indicator conjecture is recorded independently in `wiki/reports/economic-index-survey-2026-04-announcement.md` for the survey. Two Anthropic pages now claim early warning; no publication demonstrates it.
11. **Innovation, as distinct from productivity (`ED-4`).** `productivity-gains-2025-11` measures time saved on existing tasks; nothing measures invention.

*Framings used without support on this page*

12. **The inside-the-lab warrant (`Lead ¶1`, `¶3`, `¶5`) is asserted by analogy and never examined as a constraint.** The page says privileged access makes the research possible; it does not say that the data are generated by the vendor's own product, that the population is Claude users rather than workers, or that the firm has a commercial interest in the answers. Every Economic Index report carries a version of the user-base caveat — `economic-index-connector-2026-07` puts it plainly, "the Index reflects patterns in Claude usage rather than the labor market as a whole" (recorded in `wiki/reports/programme-and-product-pages.md`) — and **this page, which sets the research agenda, carries no such caveat at all.** Any post that cites the agenda as motivation should supply the caveat the agenda omits.
13. **"we can see early evidence that jobs like software engineering are changing radically" (`Lead ¶4`).** The strongest verb on the page ("radically") sits on unnamed evidence, with no number, no window and no cited publication, in a document that links eight other Anthropic pages. A reader cannot check it.
14. **The four pillars are not prioritised and carry no success criteria.** Thirty-five questions, no ranking, no sequencing, no statement of what an answer would look like, no resourcing and no owner for the economic pillar (the Frontier Red Team and Societal Impacts teams are named for two other pillars; the Economics team is named nowhere).
15. **"More granular information … at a higher cadence" (`Share 1`) is a data commitment with no schema.** No grain, no cadence figure, no release channel, no schedule. It is not possible to tell from this page whether the June 2026 telemetry change satisfies it, which is why item 2 in the ledger section above is split between the report and a steward question.
16. **The relationship to the Economic Futures programme is unstated.** `economic-futures-program-page` carries twelve research questions in two groups and `economic-futures-research-fund-2026-07` names five funding priorities (both in `wiki/reports/programme-and-product-pages.md`); this agenda's economic pillar carries twelve more. The overlap is substantial — gain-sharing, retraining, worker-level impacts — and no page in the corpus maps one list onto the other. **A `programme/THREADS.md` reader needs that mapping and will have to construct it.**
17. **"the public can help direct those changes" (`Lead ¶4`) and "We welcome feedback" (`Lead ¶9`) describe no mechanism** beyond a Fellowship application link. There is no consultation, comment route, or published response to feedback.
18. **The page's own tag is "Policy", not "Economics".** Recorded because it affects discovery: the agenda that governs Anthropic's economics research does not appear under the Economics subject tag, and the Economics team publication list is not where a reader would find it.
19. **Nothing on the page states what TAI will *not* do,** which is the usual function of an agenda's scope section. The four areas are broad enough to include most of Anthropic's research output, so the document constrains little.
