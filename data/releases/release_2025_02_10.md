## Facets or categories

- **O\*NET task** (3,513 real tasks with usage, of 18,428 distinct task statements). The unit of
  measurement. Released as free text, not by `Task ID` — the join key is the lower-cased
  statement [V6].
- **Occupation** — 974 O\*NET-SOC occupations, reached from tasks through
  `onet_task_statements.csv`. 704 of the 974 are `.00` codes; the other 270 are `.01`/`.02`
  specialisations [V9].
- **SOC major group** (occupational category) — 22 categories carry O\*NET tasks. The file
  `SOC_Structure.csv` has 23; **`55` Military Specific Occupations has no O\*NET tasks and no BLS
  row**, so every category analysis in this release has 22 rows, not 23 [V9].
- **Collaboration pattern** — five classified patterns plus `none`. Anthropic's mapping (notebook
  cell 29, and paper Table 1): automation = `directive` + `feedback loop`; augmentation =
  `task iteration` + `learning` + `validation` [V7].
- **Wage and barrier to entry** — `MedianSalary` and `JobZone` (1–5) from `wage_data.csv`, joined
  on `O*NET-SOC Code` = `SOCcode`. All 974 occupations that appear in the task file have a wage
  row (0 unmatched); 116 of the 1,090 wage rows have no O\*NET task row [V9][V12].
- **Task Type** (`Core` / `Supplemental`) and **Domain Source** are O\*NET reference facets, not
  used by the notebook or the report.

## Metrics

| metric | definition | where |
|---|---|---|
| `pct` (task) | percentage of (occupationally relevant, classified) conversations assigned to that O\*NET task; sums to 100.0000000000 across the 3,514 rows **including `none`** | `onet_task_mappings.csv` [V6] |
| `pct_occ_scaled` | Anthropic's occupation-allocated usage: `100 * (pct / n_occurrences) / sum(pct / n_occurrences)`, where `n_occurrences` is the number of distinct occupation *Titles* the task belongs to. Sums to exactly 100 over matched rows | notebook cells 6, 9 [V7] |
| `pct` (collaboration) | percentage of conversations in each pattern; the six rows sum to **84.209**, not 100 | `automation_vs_augmentation.csv` [V6] |
| automation / augmentation share | pattern `pct` ÷ **sum of the five classified patterns (81.30793074392103)**, × 100 | notebook cell 29 [V7] |
| task coverage of an occupation | share of an occupation's `onet_task_statements` rows whose task appears in `onet_task_mappings.csv` | paper Figure 4; **not in the notebook** — reimplemented [V10] |
| `bls_pct` / `claude_pct` | each column renormalised to 100 over the 22 categories, rounded to 1 dp | notebook cell 22 [V8] |

The paper's occupation-allocation rule in words (Appendix B.1): "each occupation's conversation
count is incremented by the number of conversations assigned to that task divided by the number
of occupations associated with that task" — the notebook implements exactly this [V19].

## Thresholds

- **The privacy/noise threshold is already applied upstream, before release.** Paper Appendix
  B.1: "For privacy reasons, tasks with less than 5 unique accounts or 15 conversations are
  excluded from our analysis"; Figure 4 caption: "Task usage is defined as occurrence across five
  or more unique user accounts and fifteen or more conversations" [V19]. Consequence: the 3,513
  tasks in `onet_task_mappings.csv` are the **surviving** tasks; the 14,915 absent task
  statements mean "zero *or* below threshold", and the two cannot be distinguished. There are no
  zero-`pct` rows in the file [V6].
- **Nothing else needs applying.** Unlike the 2025-09-15 and later waves (200 conversations per
  country, 100 per US state), this release has no geography and therefore no geographic
  threshold. This is a **correction to the `economic-index-data` skill**, which says flatly
  "Thresholds are not applied in the public files" — for this release the opposite holds, and the
  threshold cannot be reproduced or relaxed because the file carries no counts.
- **No suppression flag, no `not_classified` row, no cell-count column** exists in any file [V3].
  The unclassified mass in `automation_vs_augmentation.csv` is implicit: 100 − 84.209 = **15.791
  percentage points** are simply absent, and `none` (2.901) is inside the 84.209 [V6].
- The notebook applies one analyst filter of its own: `MedianSalary > 100` in the wage plot,
  which drops 4 occupations and 0.839 points of usage [V12].

## Cuts that do not exist

Verified by listing every column of every file [V3]; none of the following is a column, a value,
or derivable:

- **No geography.** No country, state, region or city anywhere. Geography starts at
  `release_2025_09_15`.
- **No time.** No date, week, month or period column. The one-week window (2024-12-16 to
  2024-12-23) exists only in the paper's Appendix E, not in any file. No trend, no change over
  time, no before/after within this release.
- **No per-conversation, per-account or per-session data.** Everything is a global percentage.
  There are **no counts at all** — not conversations, not accounts — so no confidence interval,
  standard error or cell size can be computed, and the 5-account/15-conversation threshold cannot
  be re-derived or relaxed.
- **No cross-tabs.** No task × collaboration pattern, no occupation × collaboration pattern, no
  category × collaboration pattern. `automation_vs_augmentation.csv` is six global numbers. The
  per-task cross arrives only in `release_2025_03_27`.
- **No model, platform or plan facet.** No Claude Code, no API, no Team/Enterprise, no Sonnet vs
  Opus split (the paper's §3.5 comparison is not released). Claude.ai Free and Pro only.
- **No request/use-case clusters, no skills facet, no task hierarchy levels**, no economic
  primitives (`ai_autonomy`, `task_success`, …), no AI Usage Index, no artifacts — all of these
  are later-release constructs.
- **No demographics, no firm size, no industry (NAICS)** — occupation is inferred from the task,
  never from the user.
- **No BLS wages.** `bls_employment_may_2023.csv` carries employment *counts* for 22 major groups
  only — no detailed-occupation employment, no wage, no state. Wages come only from the O\*NET
  website scrape in `wage_data.csv`.
- **No task-level BLS or employment weight**, so a task-weighted "exposure" measure must borrow
  weights from elsewhere (e.g. `labor_market_impacts/`).
- **No uncertainty, no versioning inside the file**, no `not_classified` row, no suppression flag.

## Traps

1. **`onet_task_mappings.csv` contains a row `none` with `pct` 0.4816** that matches no O\*NET
   task [V6]. The notebook's `how="left"` merge gives it `n_occurrences = NaN`, so
   `pct_occ_scaled` is NaN and pandas silently drops it from the sum — the scaled series
   renormalises over the 3,513 real tasks. If you use raw `pct` your denominator includes `none`.
2. **`automation_vs_augmentation.csv` does not sum to 100** — it sums to 84.209 [V6]. The
   automation share depends entirely on the denominator: 42.554 % over the five classified
   patterns (Anthropic's, and the published 43 %), 41.088 % including `none`, 34.600 % over 100
   [V13]. Only the first reproduces the report. This confirms the `economic-index-data` skill's
   rule for this release.
3. **The merge to occupations multiplies rows**: 3,514 mapping rows → 4,245 rows after the join,
   because 272 task statements belong to more than one occupation (up to 34) [V7]. Aggregate with
   `pct_occ_scaled`, never with `pct`, or you will multiply-count those tasks.
4. **`wage_data.MedianSalary` mixes units.** Six occupations carry an *hourly* rate in a column of
   annual salaries: Actors 17.54, Dancers 16.31, Entertainers and Performers … 15.94, Musicians
   and Singers / Musicians, Instrumental / Singers 28.15 [V3]. The notebook's `MedianSalary > 100`
   filter removes them; it also removes 0.839 points of usage, 0.784 of it Actors [V12]. Any wage
   regression that skips this filter is wrong at the bottom of the wage distribution.
5. **`-1` is a missing-value sentinel**, not a value, in `JobZone` (121 rows) and `ChanceAuto`
   (424 rows) [V3]. `JobForecast` has no negatives.
6. **`SOC_Structure.csv` cannot be joined below the major-group level.** Its codes are 7-character
   2018 SOC codes (`11-1011`) while `onet_task_statements.csv` uses 10-character O\*NET-SOC 2019
   codes (`11-1011.00`); stripping the suffix still leaves only 687 of 775 distinct base codes
   matched [V4]. That is why the notebook joins on the two-digit major group and nothing else.
   `SOC or O*NET-SOC 2019 Title` has 263 duplicate values across levels [V5].
7. **22 categories, not 23.** Military Specific Occupations (SOC 55) is in `SOC_Structure.csv`
   but has no O\*NET tasks and no BLS row [V9]. A left join on the SOC file gives 23 rows with one
   all-zero; the report shows 22.
8. **19,530 task rows but 18,428 distinct task texts** [V6]; the duplicates are exactly what
   `n_occurrences` corrects for. Never treat `onet_task_statements.csv` as one row per task.
9. **The threshold is baked in.** Absence from `onet_task_mappings.csv` means "zero or fewer than
   5 accounts / 15 conversations". Do not describe the 14,915 absent tasks as "unused".
10. **No `NA`-as-Namibia trap here** (no country column), but 658 `Task Type` /
    `Incumbents Responding` cells are genuinely blank and become NaN under a default read; the
    notebook never touches those columns, so its default `pd.read_csv` is safe. Still read with
    `keep_default_na=False` for consistency with the rest of the atlas [V3].
11. **`plots.ipynb` cell 29 references `mcolors` without importing it.** It survives only because
    `adjust_color_brightness` is called with RGB tuples, which skip the `isinstance(color, str)`
    branch. Passing a colour *name* raises `NameError` [V14].
12. **"v1" in later releases is this release.** `task_pct_v1.csv` and
    `automation_vs_augmentation_v1.csv` in `release_2025_03_27` and
    `release_2025_09_15/data/input/` are byte-identical to the files here [V15]. Do not treat a
    "v1 vs v2" comparison as two independent windows of the same vintage — v1 is December 2024
    (Claude 3.5 Sonnet), v2 is the February–March 2025 Sonnet 3.7 window.
13. **The notebook's `wage_data` aggregation uses `agg('first')` for every non-grouped column**
    (cell 27), so all columns except `pct_occ_scaled` come from an arbitrary first row within the
    `Title` group. Harmless for the plot; a trap if you reuse `plot_df` for anything else [V14].

### Where the `economic-index-data` skill is wrong or incomplete about this release

- "Thresholds are not applied in the public files" — **wrong for this release**. The 5-account /
